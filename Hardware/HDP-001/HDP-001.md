# HDP-001: Precision mount for a 0.3 MOA rangefinder

- Date: 2026-10-12.
- Status: **proposal**, for the owner's decision. Nothing ordered, nothing built.
- Kind: hardware development plan (HDP). It spans mechanics, electronics and software; each software
  piece it calls for (§7) starts its own ADR under `Firmware/docs/` when the work begins.
- Scope: how each axis is driven, which actuator and controller, and the development order.
  The laser rangefinder itself is out of scope until its form factor is known.

## 1. Requirement

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Pointing accuracy, both axes | **0.3 MOA = 87 µrad = 18″** total (13 cm at 1500 m) |
| Range | up to 1500 m, IPSC target (about 46 × 58 cm: ±32″ × ±40″ at 1500 m) |
| Base | very stable (tripod or fixed) |
| Detection (wide) camera | on the yaw stage, offset from the payload's point of aim |
| Payload on pitch | rangefinder (+ see §6.2), 3–5 kg assumed until the form factor is known |
| Yaw | continuous, through a slip ring |
| Bus | CAN through the slip ring |

**Budget split.** 18″ has to cover everything: mechanics, camera-to-bore calibration, heat shimmer
and the base. The mechanics get **≤ 8″ per axis, settled and holding**. That sets the hardware:

- an **encoder on the output** of each axis, ≥ 20 bit (≤ 1.2″ per count) with repeatability of a few
  arcseconds, closing the position loop;
- **no backlash** in the drive;
- **torsional stiffness** high enough that wind and payload torque do not move the output much
  between loop corrections: K1 ≥ ~1×10⁴ N·m/rad (1 N·m → ≤ 20″).

## 2. The key decision: a gearbox on both axes, chosen for stiffness, not torque

The brief says "gearbox only if torque is insufficient". The station's own data says otherwise. The
GM6020 had plenty of torque (breakaway 0.6–0.7 A against a 3 A limit), yet yaw could not settle
better than 0.3°. The crossed-roller bearing's breakaway friction is 6–18× its sliding friction, so
every correction from rest lurches. To hold 8″ (39 µrad) against that 0.44 N·m step, a direct drive
needs a stiffness of 0.44 / 39×10⁻⁶ ≈ 11,000 N·m/rad from its control loop alone. With yaw's inertia
(0.033 kg·m²) that is a **~90 Hz position loop**: not realistic with this mechanics.

A 100:1 strain-wave gear supplies that stiffness mechanically (size 20: K1 ≈ 1.3×10⁴ N·m/rad,
datasheet) and multiplies the motor's inertia by 10⁴ at the output, so the same friction step
deflects the output ~7″ and the output-encoder loop removes it slowly. Its own crossed-roller output
bearing also replaces the current bearing.

**Decision: zero-backlash strain-wave gearing on both axes, with the position loop closed on an
output encoder.** Precision cycloidal (Spinea TwinSpin, lost motion < 1′) is technically equivalent
but no integrated hollow CAN joint was found at this size.

## 3. Primary recommendation: integrated hollow strain-wave joint, ZeroErr eRob, on both axes

| | |
|---|---|
| What it is | Motor + strain-wave gear + crossed-roller output bearing + **servo drive** + dual absolute encoders (motor and output) in one joint, **hollow shaft** |
| Size | **eRob90** (SHG-20-class gearing, K1 ≈ 1.3×10⁴ N·m/rad) or eRob80 (SHG-17 class, 0.81×10⁴). Not eRob70: SHG-14 class, K1 0.34×10⁴, 1 N·m twists the output ~60″ |
| Ratio | 100–120:1 |
| Hollow bore | 18 mm (brochure drawings) |
| Output encoder | 19-bit absolute multi-turn (2.5″/count), 20-bit option (1.2″) |
| Accuracy | ±7″ repeatability, ±15″ absolute, for the high-precision encoder option. A reseller lists ±10″/±45″ for another variant: **confirm which applies to the part ordered** |
| Torque | eRob90: 33–52 N·m rated, 73–113 N·m peak: far beyond the ~1.5 N·m continuous / 5 N·m peak needed. The size is chosen for stiffness |
| Bus | **CANopen** (or EtherCAT), 48 V |
| Same part on both axes | yes; pitch simply does not use the bore |
| Price indication | one reseller lists eRob70 at USD 1,671; quote the 90 |

**Who writes the controller.** Nobody writes a motor controller. The joint's drive runs current,
velocity and position loops on its own output encoder. controld becomes the **CANopen master**:
- NMT start, SDO configuration, PDO mapping at boot;
- CiA 402 state machine, cyclic synchronous position (CSP): a target per axis at 200 Hz on SYNC,
  position/velocity/current/status/temperature back;
- fault and warning words mapped onto the existing fault/hold/degrade ruling.
This is a new `MotorBackend` in controld, comparable in size to the current mixed CAN backend.
CiA 402 support on the CANopen variant is not yet confirmed: confirm with ZeroErr.

**Consequences for the station software:**
- **No homing.** Both output encoders are absolute and multi-turn: a one-time zero calibration
  replaces sensorless homing, and the soft limits come from configuration, not from end stops.
- **No rest-stop park.** Park becomes a pose; Shutdown needs a holding brake on pitch if the payload
  is not balanced (owner ruling: never de-energise an unbalanced load). Ask whether the eRob has a
  brake option; balancing pitch is required either way.

**Risks to test first (§7, phase 1):**
- strain-wave hysteresis around reversal: a dual loop with an integrator can hunt in that dead zone;
- whether the ±7″ figure holds on the variant delivered;
- 18 mm bore: it passes a cable bundle (48 V, CAN, Ethernet), not a slip ring. The slip ring sits
  below the yaw joint.

## 4. Secondary recommendation: build each axis from components

| Part | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Gear | **Harmonic Drive SHG-20-2UH** hollow unit, 100–120:1 (crossed-roller output inside; K1 1.3–1.6×10⁴; 1′ accuracy). Size 17 for pitch only if the payload is light | hollow series 14–80 mm bore: the slip ring or more cable can go through |
| Motor | frameless hollow BLDC on the wave-generator input (e.g. Kollmorgen KBM class; check bore and torque) | hollow through the axis |
| Output encoder | **Netzer VLX-80** (35 mm bore, up to 23 bit, **2.5″ repeatability**, BiSS-C/SSI), or Renishaw RESOLUTE RESA30 / REXA30 (±1″ with two readheads) | better repeatability than eRob's best case |
| Drive | **Ingenia** (Everest S XCR: dual feedback, motor encoder + output encoder; Denali NET-C if its dual BiSS-C is confirmed), **CANopen**, configured in MotionLab | turnkey, closes the loop on the output encoder, CANopen like the primary |
| Budget drive | mjbots **moteus n1**: BiSS-C on aux, output encoder as position source, **CAN-FD with its own protocol** (not CANopen) | far cheaper; CAN-FD data rate through a slip ring needs testing |

Gains: larger bore, better encoder, free choice of motor. Cost: two axes of mechanical design
(motor-to-wave-generator coupling, encoder concentricity, housings, sealing), and more parts to source.

## 5. Rejected, and why

| Option | Reason |
|---|---|
| Planetary integrated actuators (CyberGear, CubeMars AK, RobStride, MyActuator RMD) | backlash of arc-minutes |
| Hollow direct-drive gimbal BLDC with integrated driver (CubeMars GL, LK-TECH) | no loop on an output encoder: the GM6020 failure again |
| Harmonic Drive FHA-C mini | 14-bit output encoder (79″/count) |
| Harmonic Drive FHA-C | motor-side encoder only |
| MyActuator RH | 17-bit, repeatability < 0.01° (36″): marginal |

## 6. What the brief did not cover

1. **The gearbox criterion is stiffness, not torque** (§2).
2. **A narrow camera on the pitch stage, aligned with the rangefinder.** The wide camera resolves
   0.72 mrad per pixel = 2.5 MOA; a 46 cm target at 1500 m is under half a pixel. It cannot find the
   target, nor tell 0.3 MOA of aim, at that range. 0.3 MOA needs a sensor that sees the target and the
   bore together: a narrow camera (~15–30 µrad per pixel, a 200–300 mm lens) on the payload.
   The wide camera on yaw stays for search.
3. **A yaw-mounted camera changes the kinematics.** Today both cameras ride the pitch stage
   (`R_P_C`). A camera on the yaw stage does not tilt with pitch, so its relation to the bore changes
   with the pitch angle: a fixed offset cannot express it. Each camera needs to name the link it rides
   on (yaw or pitch) and its extrinsic on that link; the solver then aims the bore, carried by pitch,
   at a line of sight measured from the yaw frame. Software work, listed in §7.
4. **What crosses the slip ring.** Camera links (CSI, USB 3) do not survive a slip ring. Put the Pi
   and the Hailo on the yaw stage with the cameras; the ring then carries **48 V, CAN to the yaw joint
   (whose body sits on the base), and Ethernet** (or use Wi-Fi). Classic CAN at 1 Mbit/s with two
   joints at 200 Hz is ~20% bus load, with margin for a slip ring's contacts.
5. **Power.** The joints are 48 V: a supply sized for both peaks, separate from the 5 V for the Pi.
   This also ends the current 5 V droop.
6. **Brake and balance** for the heavy-payload shutdown (§3).
7. **Calibration procedure for 0.3 MOA:** each camera's offset (now one per camera in
   `alignment.camera_offset`), the rangefinder-to-narrow-camera boresight (the 1535 nm beam is
   invisible: range a small target at distance while scanning, or use a camera that sees 1.5 µm),
   and the encoder zero.
8. **Heat shimmer at the venue** can be tens of µrad: measure it with the narrow camera before
   paying for more precision.

## 7. Development plan

| Phase | Work | Exit criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Owner: rangefinder and narrow-camera choice, so payload mass, CG and size are known | payload envelope fixed |
| 1 | **One eRob90 on the bench** with a dummy payload. controld CANopen backend (CiA 402 CSP). Measure with an independent reference (narrow camera on a target 20–50 m away: 8″ is 1–2 mm at 50 m): step settling, hold jitter, reversal hunting, response to a torque disturbance | **≤ 8″ settled and holding, no limit cycle**. Otherwise the secondary path |
| 2 | Two axes, slip ring, Pi + Hailo on the yaw stage, 48 V supply, balanced pitch, brake decision | both axes meet phase 1 on the real structure |
| 3 | Software: camera-per-link kinematics, narrow camera tracking, camera offsets calibrated, rangefinder interface | aim verified at 100 m |
| 4 | Field test to 1500 m | 0.3 MOA on target |

The secondary path follows the same phases with its own drive configuration in place of the CANopen
backend work.
