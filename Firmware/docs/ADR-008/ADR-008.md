# ADR-008: Running indefinitely — every state bounded, every failure contained

- **Date:** 2026-11-10 (NZDT).
- **Status:** **Proposed.** Written before any code change, at the owner's request. The owner
  answered section 6's questions on 2026-11-10 (restart in Shutdown; V3b may run). Section 7 says how each stage is proven on the
  station, and section 9 says when this ADR closes.
- **Request (owner, 2026-11-10):** after the 13:13 stop, address it "from a system perspective, i.e.
  what design can make the turret controller software run indefinitely by design", looking at the
  software design and at storage. Propose a solution and write it up as an ADR before changing code.

## 1. What happened (station, 2026-11-10)

The stack ran for 910,545 s (10.5 days, from the boot service's start on 2026-10-31 00:17). At
**13:13:24 NZDT** visiond died, and the launcher shut the whole station down. The shutdown was
clean: yaw to 0, pitch onto its rest stop, both motors off.

1. Perception gives each new track a label, "Person #N". The counter goes up for the life of the
   visiond process and is never reused (`perception/tracking/track_manager.py:197`, "§27:
   monotonic within a session"). That rule came from a real bug: reusing the lowest free label made
   `select_target 2` answer "selected Person #1".
2. The TrackSet datagram to controld carries the label as `uint16`. When it passed 65,535,
   `perception/protocol/wire.py:293` raised `ValueError: display index exceeds legacy wire
   capacity; refusing label reuse`. The last published track was `person #65536`.
   - The scene creates about 6,300 tracks a day (65,839 in 10.5 days), mostly short tracks that are
     lost and created again.
   - At that rate, the overflow is a fault that goes off after a fixed uptime.
3. Nothing caught the exception. The wide camera's publish call sits outside the frame's catch-all
   (`perception/visiond.py:1033`, while `pipeline.py:644` guards the rest). The detail-camera
   worker catches the same calls (`visiond.py:930`).
4. The launcher waits on controld, webd and visiond (`scripts/run_application.sh:912-916`). The
   first one to exit stops the stack: `cause=child_exit exited_child=visiond wait_status=1`.
5. Nothing brought it back. The launcher then exited with **status 0**: `systemctl --user status`
   shows `ExecStart … status=0/SUCCESS`. The unit has `Restart=no`, and even `on-failure` would
   not have fired on a success.

So one counter took down a process that does not own safety. That process took down the process
that does, and the station then stayed down until a person came. **Each of those three steps is a
design gap of its own.** The label is only the first instance of the first one.

## 2. The invariants a forever-running station needs

The stack is meant to run for months, unattended, on a Pi with 8 GB of RAM, a 4 GB tmpfs `/tmp` and
an SD card. "Indefinitely" is not a soak test that passed. It is a property of the design: every
quantity that grows has a bound, and every failure has a way back. Four invariants say that:

- **I1 — Bounded state.** Every value that grows with uptime has a bound and a defined rule at the
  bound. Reaching the bound is not an error.
  - A counter that crosses into a fixed-width field either wraps by a rule its consumers accept, or
    is wide enough never to be reached (64 bits at these rates).
  - A collection is pruned when it grows, not when someone happens to read it.
- **I2 — Bounded storage.** Every byte a running stack writes has a budget, and the run dir as a
  whole has one.
  - Hitting a budget rotates or prunes; it never fails a writer, and a writer never dies because a
    write failed.
  - Durable state survives a power cut. The station has a marginal supply (see ADR-007, section 2),
    so this is not hypothetical.
- **I3 — Contained failure.** controld owns motor safety, and only its exit ends the stack.
  - **The other processes fail alone and come back by themselves.** They are visiond, webd, calibd
    and the observe-only IMU capture.
  - **An error in one message or one frame drops that message, not the process.** This is the
    owner's 2026-10-02 ruling applied to software: a transient degrades, a persistent non-hazard
    holds and recovers by itself, and only a hazard faults (see the "Fault, hold, degrade" section
    in [`STATION_OPERATIONS.md`](../STATION_OPERATIONS.md)).
  - **A crash of the whole stack restarts it** in the state section 6, question 1 decides.
- **I4 — Visible.** Growth that is still within its bound is measured and shown, so a leak is seen
  long before it bites. 13:13 was the first anyone knew of a counter that had been climbing for ten
  days.

## 4. Decisions (proposed)

### D1. A counter that crosses a fixed-width field wraps by a rule; it never raises

**The label.** The per-class counter runs 1 → 65,535 and then back to 1. When it hands out a label,
it skips any label still held by a live (not retired) track of that class, and the selected
target's. At most 32 tracks are live, so a free label is always found within 33 steps. 0 stays
reserved, because the HUD and controld read 0 as "no target" (`web/webd/hud.py:386`,
`control_loop.cpp:7757`).

The invariant that §27 actually needs is "a label is not reused while anyone can still mean the old
holder", not "never". After a wrap, a label comes back only after more than 65,500 other tracks of
its class, about ten days here. The 10-02 bug reused a label within seconds. The docstring and §27's
wording change to say this.

Rejected alternatives:
- **A new session (new `session_uuid`, labels back to 1) at the limit.** It invalidates the
  operator's selection and controld's at a random moment, perhaps mid-track. It disturbs tracking
  every ten days to protect an invariant that the wrap already keeps.
- **Widening the wire field to 32 bits.** It changes controld's fixed 2,946-byte struct on both
  sides, for labels like "Person #400,000" that nobody can read. The wrap stays local to the
  producer.

**The rule for the rest.**
- `cycles` in `main.cpp` becomes `int64_t`.
- Every fixed-width field in the perception wire either clamps (it already does for ages and
  frames) or wraps by a stated rule. A raise remains only for a programming error. Even then, D3
  catches it at the frame.

### D2. Supervision: only controld ends the stack; the rest restart by themselves

The launcher's single `wait -n` becomes a supervision loop with two classes of child:

| Class | Children | On exit |
|---|---|---|
| **core** | controld | The stack stops, as today: the remaining children get TERM, and controld will already have parked if it could. The launcher then **exits non-zero**, so systemd restarts it (D4). |
| **service** | visiond, webd, calibd, IMU capture | The launcher restarts the child with backoff: 1, 2, 4 … s, capped at 60 s, and reset after 10 minutes up. It never gives up: a crash loop at the 60 s cap is harmless and visible. Each restart is logged with the exit status, and the child's log gets a marker line. |

controld already handles each service going away:
- visiond gone is "vision lost". The tracker loses the target and returns to AUTO_ROAM or the watch
  point, and that is a HOLD at most, per the ruling.
- webd gone is a page that reconnects.
- calibd gone fails the run in progress.

**The station stays homed through any of these.** That is the main gain: today a visiond crash
costs a full shutdown and a person to HOME it.

While webd restarts, the page's STOP is unavailable for a few seconds. The launcher's `stop` (over
SSH) and the boot service's stop are unaffected.

The loop also gives the launcher a housekeeping tick every 10 s, which D5 and D6 use. Bash 5.2 is on
the station, so `wait -n -p` reports which child exited, and a ticker child in the wait set provides
the tick. No new language or daemon is needed.

### D3. Per-message errors drop the message

- **The wide loop's publish.** It gets the same catch as the detail worker: drop the frame's wire
  message, count `wire_failures`, and log the first one and then one summary a minute.
  - A single bad frame is a DEGRADE.
  - An error on every frame leaves controld without vision. It holds by its own vision-stale rules
    and recovers when frames encode again. visiond stays up, so the page still shows video.
- **`publish_timing`** writes a non-finite value as `null`.
- **webd's controld reader** catches everything per message and keeps reading. If its socket fails,
  it reconnects, with backoff.
- **calibd** catches `OSError` around send, write and prune, and fails that run instead of dying.

### D4. The boot service restarts a crashed stack

`ota-station.service` gets `Restart=on-failure` and `RestartSec=10`. The launcher's exit code
carries the cause:

| Cause | Exit code |
|---|---|
| `operator_stop`, `external_signal` (a systemd stop, a deploy) | 0, no restart |
| a core exit (`child_exit` of controld), `unattributed` | 1, restart |

The restarted stack comes up in the state question 1 decides. The recommendation is **Shutdown**, as
at boot.

The restart does not change what the motors did when controld died: that is the drives' own
host-silence behaviour. The GM6020 zeroes its own output about 200 ms after the host goes quiet;
what the CyberGear does with no host at all is to be confirmed. The restart only brings back the web,
the camera and a controller that can be told to HOME.

### D5. Storage budgets, enforced in place

**Each redirected log** opens in append mode (`>>`, `O_APPEND`). On each housekeeping tick, a log over
its cap is rotated in place: its tail is copied to `<name>.1` and the file is truncated (copytruncate).
This works for every writer, including output from native libraries (libcamera, HailoRT). It needs
no pipe and no extra process, so it adds no `SIGPIPE` risk to controld. At most one tick's lines are
lost at a rotation.

| File | Cap (×2 with `.1`) | History it keeps at today's rate |
|---|---|---|
| `controller.log` | 64 MiB | about 1.6 days idle (status block) |
| `vision.log` | 16 MiB | about 7 days |
| `web.log`, `calibd.log`, `imu.log`, `launcher.log` | 8 MiB | months |

**`track-motion` leaves `controller.log`.** At 100 Hz it is a trace, not a log, and the 200 Hz
`tracking-trace.jsonl` exists for exactly that. First check that the trace carries the same fields
(q, v, qref, vref, aref, q_p, qref_p). Add any that are missing to the trace, then drop the line.
With it gone, the cap's history stays in days even while tracking.

**`logs-history/` gets a byte budget of 256 MiB**, pruned oldest first at each start and each tick.
The count limit stays as well.

**A run-dir budget of 768 MiB in all** (19 % of the tmpfs, under 10 % of RAM). Each tick measures
the total. Above it, the tick prunes `logs-history` first, then the `.1` files. The per-file caps
above sum to well under the budget, so this is a backstop, not the mechanism.

**Smaller fixes:**
- The IMU capture retries its truncation after a failed `fflush` instead of giving up retention.
- Trip files are kept to the newest 50 per run.
- `cleanup` writes its small files with `|| true`, so nothing it writes can stop it from signalling
  the children.

**Crash evidence survives a reboot.** A core exit, or a service restart, writes a small bundle to
`run/evidence/<stamp>-<cause>/` on the SD card: `shutdown.cause`, the exit status, and the last
1 MiB of each log. It keeps the newest 20 bundles, 200 MiB at most. Today's evidence survived only
because nobody rebooted.

**Durable state** is written as: temp file, fsync it, rename it, fsync the directory. This applies to
`watch_point.json`, `target_type.json`, and calibd's `manifest.json` and `run.json`, which also become
atomic. They are rare writes, so this costs no meaningful SD wear.

### D6. Leaks fixed at their source, and resource use made visible

- `client_threads_` in both servers is reaped: finished threads are joined at each accept.
- webd's preview timestamp lists are trimmed to the 5 s window on append.
- `AliasMap` drops an alias when its survivor retires, since nothing can resolve it after that.
- **A resource watch.** Each minute the housekeeping tick writes `resources.json`. It holds, for each
  child, its RSS, thread count, open file count, uptime and restarts, plus the run dir's bytes, the
  tmpfs's free space and the SD's free space. The tick also appends the same as one line to
  `resources.ndjson` (capped like the logs, about 4 MiB a month). `run_application.sh status` prints
  it. Showing it on the page (MENU › stats) is a small follow-up, not part of this ADR.

## 5. Alternatives considered

- **systemd unit per process** (controld, visiond, webd, …), with `Restart=` and journald for logs.
  This is the standard industrial answer, and it was rejected for now:
  - The launcher owns things a unit graph would have to rebuild: the controlled park, the start
    order, the run dir, the Homed/Shutdown state and the deploy's stop/start.
  - journald on the station is volatile, and its rate limiter would drop exactly the bursts worth
    keeping.
  - Its configuration is the owner's sudo.
  - D2 gives the same containment inside the one supervised unit the station already trusts.
  - **What reopens this:** a second core process, or a need for cgroup memory limits per process.
- **A log pipe through a rotating helper** (`multilog`, `rotatelogs` style). It rotates exactly, but
  a dead helper sends `SIGPIPE` or `EPIPE` to every writer. For controld that is a crash in the
  safety process. Copytruncate has no such coupling.
- **spdlog's rotating sink inside controld.** It is exact for controld, but it does nothing for
  Python and native-library output. One mechanism for all logs is simpler, and it can be revisited
  if controld's log needs exact rotation.
- **Lowering the 1 Hz status block.** Not needed once the cap holds. It is the owner's evidence
  stream, and it stays.

## 6. Owner decisions

**Answered 2026-11-10:**
- **Question 1:** after a controld crash, restart in **Shutdown**.
- **Question 2:** **yes, V3b may run** on the station (homed, AUTO_ROAM), and the station is left as
  found.
- The station was restarted the same afternoon. It came back through `ota-station.service` in
  Shutdown, from release `00add4f`, which still has the label overflow, about ten days out again.
- Question 3 (the budgets) was not raised; they stand as proposed.

The questions as they were put:

1. **After controld itself crashes, should the stack restart by itself?** Recommended: yes, in
   **Shutdown**. That is the boot state of the 2026-10-03 ruling, so web and camera come back and the
   operator can see why and HOME it remotely. Alternatives:
   - Restart and re-home if it was homed. The station keeps working, but it moves motors unattended
     after a crash of the safety process.
   - Stay down, as today. Nothing is visible until someone comes.
2. **Station tests that interrupt processes (section 7).** Two kinds:
   - V3 and V4, with the motors off in Shutdown, carry no motion risk.
   - **V3b kills visiond while the station is homed and in AUTO_ROAM.** That is the case this ADR
     exists for, and it needs the owner's yes. ADR-005's deploy autonomy does not cover it.
3. **The budgets** in D5 (64 MiB controller log, 768 MiB run dir, 20 evidence bundles) are a
   proposal, sized from the rates measured above. Change them if you need longer history.

## 7. Stages, and how each is proven

The stages are integrated one at a time. Each is deployed with `deploy_station.py --prebuilt` and
proven on the station before the next one starts. Nobody waits ten days: the bounds are reached on
purpose.

| Stage | Contents | Proof (engineering pass: no crash, bound held, nothing lost that matters) |
|---|---|---|
| **S1 — the crash itself** | D1 (label wrap, `int64` cycles), D3 | Unit tests start the label counter at 65,530 and run 100 tracks through the encoder: no exception, no label shared by two live tracks, 0 never issued. Encoder test: an encode error drops one message and counts it. On the station: the stack runs, homed, tracking as before. |
| **S2 — supervision** | D2, D4 | **V3** (Shutdown, motors off): `kill -KILL` visiond, then webd, then calibd. Each is back within its backoff; controld keeps its pid and logs no stop; the page comes back. **V3b** (homed, AUTO_ROAM, owner's yes): kill visiond. The station stays homed and roams, video and tracking return, nothing de-energises. **V4** (Shutdown): kill controld. systemd restarts the stack in Shutdown within about 20 s, and `run/evidence/` holds the bundle. **V5** (Shutdown): kill the launcher (section 8). |
| **S3 — storage** | D5 | On the station, the caps are set to 1 MiB for 15 minutes (the caps are read from the environment): every log stays under cap plus one tick of growth, `.1` holds the tail, no NUL bytes after a truncation, no process restarts. The total run dir stays under budget with `logs-history` artificially filled. A power cut is not simulated: the fsync order is checked by review. |
| **S4 — leaks and visibility** | D6 | Tests for the thread reaping and the list trimming. On the station: `resources.json` is updated each minute, and webd and visiond reconnect 50 times with flat thread counts. |

After S4, an independent reviewer gets the code, not these conclusions, and looks first at the
failure paths. This step follows the owner's 2026-10-25 practice.

## 8. Failure modes of this design

- **A crash loop hides behind restarts.** A service that dies every minute "works". The restart
  count is in `resources.json` and in `status`. A rising count with no human action is the signal,
  and the evidence bundles keep the why.
- **Copytruncate loses a few lines** at each rotation (at most one tick's). It is accepted for logs.
  It must never be applied to a trace whose reader expects complete records; the traces keep their
  own rotating writers.
- **The label wrap reuses a label after about ten days.** A selection that old should not still be
  live. Not yet read in the code: S1 checks that controld drops a selected label once it leaves the
  TrackSet, and that a later track given the same label is not taken for the old one.
- **A restart races a deploy.** `deploy_station.py` stops through the launcher (`operator_stop`,
  exit 0), so systemd does not restart behind it. V4 also covers a deploy right after a crash
  restart.
- **The supervision loop is itself the single point of failure.** It is bash with no state beyond
  pids and backoff counters. What happens to controld when the launcher itself is killed has not
  been read or tested: `KillMode=mixed` signals only the launcher on a systemd stop. S2 adds a
  **V5**, run in Shutdown: kill the launcher, then confirm the children are stopped (controld parks)
  and systemd restarts the stack, with no orphaned controld left holding the CAN buses.

## 9. Closing condition, and what reopens it

**It closes when:**
- S1–S4 are deployed, and each has passed its proof on the station (V3b only with the owner's yes);
- the independent review's findings are fixed;
- the [start/stop/status card](../operations/start-stop-status.md) states the restart policy, the
  budgets and the evidence directory;
- the runbook's guard table has a row for "a service process exits" (DEGRADE: restart).

**It reopens if:**
- a `resources.ndjson` slope projects any bound to be reached within a year;
- a second process needs to be core;
- a new fixed-width field joins a wire format without a stated rule at its bound.
