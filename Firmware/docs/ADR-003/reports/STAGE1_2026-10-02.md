# ADR-003 stage 1: math, interfaces and the 14 scenarios in simulation (2026-10-02)

Plan and decisions: [IMPLEMENTATION.md](../IMPLEMENTATION.md). Reproduce with
`tools/tracking/tracking.py stage1` (a few seconds). Test suites:
- `test-tracking`: tracking_core unit checks;
- `tools/tracking/tests`: the scenarios and the FF/FB comparison;
- `tools/servo_commission/tests`: the servo pipeline.

All three pass.

## What was built

**`tracking_core/`** (C++20, no dependencies beyond the existing geometry headers) holds three
pieces:
- **`estimator.*`** (sec. 3–4) runs one CV Kalman filter per LOS axis with continuous-time process
  noise q_a. Its rules:
  - one update per frame, Joseph form, wrapped azimuth innovation;
  - repeated or older frames are rejected;
  - when NIS exceeds 9.21, process noise is scaled once by min(scale_max, NIS/9.21), which decays
    back with 2 T_m;
  - robust update with R/w;
  - reacquire after two frames outside the rate domain.

  The query is constant (it never advances the state). It returns one motion hypothesis, with
  the confidence weight w = smoothstep((|ω|/σ − 1)/2) and the cubic coast fade g(age) and its
  analytic integral, both applied. There is no acceleration state.
- **`level1.*`** (sec. 5) computes a = λ²(q_t − q_r) + 2λ(v_t − v_r), then constrains it in a
  fixed order:
  - the lead limit, with hysteresis;
  - the travel boundary, by clamping and braking;
  - the speed ceiling, with an a²/2j reserve;
  - the acceleration bound;
  - the jerk bound.

  It publishes one constant-jerk segment per tick. Event-split integration means a late tick can
  exceed neither a_max nor v_max.
- **`tracker.*`** turns a pixel into a LOS:
  - pixel at the optical time t_o = SensorTimestamp + offset + exposure/2 + row term;
  - pose at t_o;
  - base-frame LOS through the existing camera model and kinematics, with the pixel noise
    propagated through the same Jacobian.

  Each tick, it queries the estimator at t + h_exec. The joint goal comes from the existing
  LOS-joint solver near the current reference branch, with v = J⁻¹ω (zero near a singularity).
  Level 1 then produces the reference sample.

**The parameter asset** has two parts:
- **The loader** (`tracking_config.*`) requires every key; a missing one fails the load. It writes
  a read-back that round-trips.
- **The prior** is [`config/tracking/tracking_prior.json`](../../../config/tracking/tracking_prior.json),
  stage 1's explicit synthetic values. Each one's source is written in it.

**`tracking-sim`** (`tracking_sim.*`) is the whole chain with the production classes:
- **Target truth:** piecewise-constant acceleration, jumps and identity changes.
- **Camera:** period and jitter, exposure, a timestamp at exposure start (with an optional wrong
  offset), noise, latency and its jitter, drops and extra delay. Delivery is latest-only.
- **Tracker.**
- **Yaw:** the ADR-002.2 `Servo` on every 1 kHz encoder receipt against `YawPlant` (LuGre friction,
  crosstalk, delay).
- **Pitch:** `PositionLoop` against `PitchPlant`, using the commissioned assets.
- **An ideal actuator,** to test Level 1 alone.

The outputs are the true framing error (the true target through the true camera pose at
mid-exposure), the blur over the exposure, and every layer of the error.

**`tools/tracking/`** holds the scenario harness:
- `scenarios.py`: the 14 scenarios;
- `evaluate.py`: framing, lag, settle, e_track / e_servo / e_goal, reference consistency and
  bounds;
- `stage1.py`: per-scenario property checks and the comparison;
- `tracking.py stage1`: the report.

## Results (prior parameters, simulated camera at about 9 fps, 60 ms latency, 3 px noise)

All 14 scenarios pass all 83 checks. Each check tests the property the scenario must show (the
"必须证明" column), with bounds derived from design quantities: H 0.75 s, 4/λ 1 s, the pixel noise,
and the reference's own stop and braking distance. Highlights:

- **Camera rotation is not target motion.** T01 is a stationary subject while the turret slews
  8°. The estimated base-frame rate stayed within 3σ of zero throughout.
- **Static hold.** T02: 1.8 px RMS framing at 3 px anchor noise; FF weight below 0.05.
- **Stop and reversal.**
  - T07: the stop was recognised in 0.19 s and settled within H + stop + 4/λ. The overshoot is
    reported, not gated: no model can know a stop before it is seen.
  - T08: the target FF changed sign once, and the reference reversed once (no chase in the old
    direction).
- **Degradation.**
  - T10: FF exactly zero while velocity was unavailable, the axis kept following on position
    feedback, and the lag returned to the earlier level after restoration.
  - T11: delayed frames were used at their optical time; out-of-order ones were rejected.
  - T12: the control tick never waited for vision, and the subject was held through a 0.4 s drop.
  - T13: when the axis was stuck, the reference stopped within its lead limit plus braking
    distance and held. It resumed toward the current target with no replay.
  - T14: the target became invalid after the horizon, the reference held, and the new identity
    started with zero velocity.
- **The reference is one integral.** Every published q/v/a equals the previous segment integrated
  (residual 0), within its v/a/j limits.

**ADR-003 sec. 7A, Level-1 FF+FB against FB only** (same data, steady windows):

| Scenario | Rate | Framing FF+FB / FB only | Lag FF+FB / FB only |
|---|---|---|---|
| T04 fast constant | 20 deg/s | 15.8 / 297 px | 0.002 / 0.64 s |
| T05 after acceleration | 20 deg/s | 18.7 / 309 px | 0.00 / 0.64 s |
| T08 after reversal | -15 deg/s | 22.7 / 241 px | 0.007 / 0.64 s |
| T10 after FF restored | 8 deg/s | 2.9 / 126 px | 0.005 / 0.64 s |
| T03 slow constant | 2 deg/s | 24.4 / 30.4 px | 0.50 / 0.63 s |

**Slow subjects are limited by velocity confidence.** At 2°/s the rate is below 3σ of the
velocity estimate (σ_ω ≈ 1–3°/s with these priors at 9 fps). The confidence weight therefore
keeps the FF mostly off, and the reference trails by about 2v/λ. This is the designed behaviour
for an uncertain rate. Stage 2 fits q_a and the pixel noise from data, and solves λ, which
together set where full FF begins.

**Level 2 adds little in simulation.** The ideal actuator's framing is within 0.1–1 px of the
servo's in every scenario except T13, where the axis is deliberately stuck. The servo model's
tracking is optimistic, though: the station's own servo error is in
[`yaw_accuracy.json`](../../../config/servo/yaw_accuracy.json). Stage 3 measures the real split.

## Corrections found while building it

1. **An integer overflow in a test.** `k*5'000'000` in `int` made the optimiser loop forever. The
   tests use 64-bit time.
2. **The lead limit first pulled the reference back to plant + L.** That turns into a carrot that
   limits normal acquisition speed to about λL/2. Now the reference holds, and releases at half
   the limit.
3. **The first stage-1 checks invented performance numbers,** for example 6 px framing at 2°/s
   when the FF is correctly suppressed. They were rewritten as property checks with derived
   bounds. The numbers are reported, not gated.
4. **Two scenarios were mis-specified.** T09 injected 6 px noise while telling the tracker 3 px,
   so the estimator's R was wrong by 2×. T14's loss window was shorter than the horizon plus
   settling, so the hold could not be observed.
