# ADR-005: Calibration records — indexed payload sets, chosen from the web

- **Date:** 2026-10-24 (NZDT).
- **Status:** Proposed. The owner's decisions in section 2 are taken; nothing is implemented.
- **Request (owner, 2026-10-24):** several calibration records, indexed. From the web: select one,
  rename it, re-run the calibration of the current one, add a new one and run it, adjust it by hand.
  A switch follows the target-type switch policy.
- **Builds on:** the target-type switch ([DESIGN_TARGET_TYPE_2026-10-20.md](../DESIGN_TARGET_TYPE_2026-10-20.md)),
  servo commissioning ([card](../operations/servo-commissioning.md)), and the fault/hold, two-state
  and web-page rulings in the [runbook](../STATION_OPERATIONS.md).

## 1. Context

### What exists today

There is one copy of everything. Each calibration is one file at a fixed path, in git, shipped with
every release:

| Part | File | Read by | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Yaw servo asset | `config/servo/yaw_servo.json` | controld's backend (`load_servos`, `mixed_can_motor_backend.cpp`) | once, at backend open |
| Pitch servo asset | `config/servo/pitch_servo.json` | the same | the same |
| Yaw accuracy limits | `config/servo/yaw_accuracy.json` | commissioning only (`score.py`); no production process reads it | — |
| Camera→pitch extrinsic | `calibration/camera_extrinsics.yaml` | controld (`tracking_setup.hpp`); placed into each power-up's pitch frame after homing | at start; startup-only |
| Bore offsets | `alignment.camera_offset.{wide,detail}` in `config/turret_mixed.yaml` | controld's tracker and the HUD bore mark (via telemetry) | at start; `set_tracking_config` throws if they change while tracking |

A payload change today means running `commission.py` from the workstation, committing the asset and
deploying. Going back to an earlier payload means digging in git or commissioning again. The only
mechanism with several copies is the v1 `config/payload_profiles/` (named profiles,
`payload.active_profile`), which is dormant on the mixed station and unreachable from the page.

### Facts that shape the design (read from the code, 2026-10-24)

1. **Commissioning cannot run beside production.** Both take `/tmp/ota-motion-$UID.lock`
   (`run_application.sh:355`). The session supervisor also refuses while controld or imu-bno085
   exists (`adr0022_capture_launch.py:209-224`). One process drives the CAN buses.
2. **The page goes down with production.** webd is a child of the production launcher, and
   `wait -n` ends the whole run when any child exits (`run_application.sh:835-841`).
3. **Everything a run needs already ships to the Pi, except scipy.** Every prebuilt release carries
   arm64 `commissiond`, `servo-sim` and `imu-bno085`. numpy is in `requirements-station.txt`;
   scipy is not.
4. **The workstation's share of a run is small.** The design step takes 10-15 s per yaw run, and
   all the computation about a minute. Station time dominates (workstation-run file times):
   - `yaw --update`: 19 min of sessions;
   - `pitch`: 25 min, not the card's 10; the extra time is outside commissiond and unexplained;
   - `calibrate yaw`: 22 min of sessions plus 4 min of scoring.

   A full yaw run writes about 1 GB of journals.
5. **Reconfiguring an engaged yaw servo trips it.** `Servo::configure` clears its ready flag, and
   the next step latches `servo_data_invalid`. A bumpless path exists, though:
   - `release_yaw_servo_locked` releases the servo;
   - the next 5 ms tick re-engages it through `command_reference`;
   - `Servo::reset` seeds the integral from the applied current.

   Pitch re-engages with its integral at zero, as every engage does today. Meanwhile the drive
   holds speed zero on its own encoder.
6. **The geometry has a natural seam.** In the MANUAL hold there is no tracker. Resuming a mode
   rebuilds the tracker from `tracking_cfg_` (`enable_tracking`). `place_extrinsic_in_session`
   places the extrinsic in the power-up's frame after homing.
7. **There is no code that fits the extrinsic.** The 2026-10-05 rotation of −41.765° was computed
   by hand from `stationary_trial.py` runs. The later re-references to the homed frame were hand
   edits.
8. **The target-type switch is specific to target type** (`TargetSwitch`, `step_target_switch`,
   `control_loop.cpp:4695-4748`). Writing this ADR found a gap in it: STOP MOTION or HOLD during a
   switch did not clear the remembered mode, so the turret resumed AUTO_ROAM when the swap
   completed, against the operator's last command. It was fixed on 2026-10-24, in the commit that
   adds this ADR (test `TargetTypeSwitch.AStopDuringTheSwapIsNotUndoneWhenItEnds`).

## 2. Owner decisions (2026-10-24)

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| What a record holds | A **payload set**: everything that changes when what sits on the pitch arm changes. |
| Running a calibration | **On the station**, from the web button: identify, design and verify on the Pi. The page stays up. |
| Manual adjustment | **Aim trims only.** The bore offsets, in clicks, as on a scope. Servo numbers are read-only. |
| Storage | **A station store** in `run/state/calibration/`, which survives deploys. Today's committed files become record #1. A tool copies records back into the repository. |
| Switching | The target-type policy: drop the target, hold energised, swap, resume. Overdue at 10 s → HOLD, no fault. |

## 3. Decisions

### D1. A record is a payload set

| Part | Why it belongs to the payload |
|---|---|
| Yaw servo asset | inertia, friction, and the gains designed from them |
| Pitch servo asset | the speed loop's gain under this load, and the gains |
| Yaw accuracy limits | measured with this payload's servo |
| Camera→pitch extrinsic | changes when the camera's mounting on the arm changes |
| Bore offsets (wide, detail) | where each camera looks relative to the payload's point of aim |

These stay shared, because they are station facts, not payload facts:

- lens intrinsics;
- the camera mount orientation (`camera_install.yaml`);
- homing contacts (retained per power-up) and the pitch working window from homing;
- the installation pose;
- the watch point, the target type and the live speeds.

The yaw asset keeps its encoder crosstalk table, although the table is a property of the motor, not
the payload. Splitting it out would break the asset's single provenance. The consequence is in
risk 5.

### D2. Index and name

- **Id.** An integer assigned at creation, never reused, shown as `#N` (as tracks are). The id is
  the identity: commands and the persisted choice use it.
- **Name.** A label of up to 24 characters (letters, digits, space, `-`, `_`), shown upper case.
  It can be edited at any time, and two records may share a name.
- **Record #1** is seeded from the release's committed files and named `AS SHIPPED`.
- **No delete from the page.** Deleting is irreversible and a record is small. The store tool can
  prune.

### D3. Storage: the station store

```
run/state/calibration/
  active.json                   {"schema": 1, "active": 2}
  records/0002/record.json      name, parts, provenance
  records/0002/yaw_servo.json  pitch_servo.json  yaw_accuracy.json  camera_extrinsics.yaml  bore.yaml
  records/0002/history/<time>/  every part that a run or a trim replaced
  records/0002/runs/<run id>/   commission.log, report.json, report.md, session journals
```

- **Location.** `deploy_station.py` already links `run/state` into every release. It survives
  deploys and reboots, as `watch_point.json` and `target_type.json` do.
- **`record.json` is the record's manifest**, in the spirit of `calibration/manifest.yaml`. For
  each part it lists the file, its sha256 and its origin:
  - `measured`: run id and date;
  - `inherited`: from record #k;
  - `trimmed`;
  - `shipped`: git revision.

  It also carries the frame its extrinsic is in (`pitch_from_homed_low`).
- **Writes are atomic** (temp file, then rename), with one writer at a time. That writer is
  controld, except during a run, when the run holds the motion lock and is the only writer (D7).
- **What the station keeps.** It never deletes history. It keeps the journals of the latest
  accepted run per record, because `calibrate yaw` needs the asset's own validation passes. Older
  journals are pruned and their reports kept.

**Seeding and import.** At start, before controld, the launcher compares the release's committed set
by hash with every record. The committed set is the servo assets, the accuracy limits, the
extrinsic and `alignment.camera_offset`.

- An empty store gets record #1, `AS SHIPPED`, which becomes active.
- A committed set that matches no record becomes a new record, `RELEASE <rev>`, and is **not**
  activated.

So the workstation flow (commission, commit, deploy) still works, and its result appears as a chip.
Nothing a deploy brings is ever activated by itself. From then on production reads only the active
record. The committed files are a seed and an import source, not a second truth.

**Back to the repository.** `tools/calibration_records.py pull`, run on the workstation, copies
records without their journals into `Firmware/calibration/records/<id>-<name>/`, where they are
committed as evidence. Journals stay runtime captures, outside git.

### D4. Which record is in force

- controld reads `active.json` at start. It loads that record's parts before it builds the tracking
  configuration and opens the backend (`main.cpp`, around lines 209 and 269).
- **An active record that is missing or fails its hashes** brings controld up in Shutdown with no
  servo configured. HOME is refused with the reason, for example "record #2: yaw_servo.json does
  not match its hash; choose a record".
  - controld never falls back to another record by itself. A servo designed for another payload is
    the very failure this feature exists to prevent.
  - Boot is Shutdown, so nothing moves, and this is not a fault.
- **The page always shows the record in force**: `#N NAME` in the header line, beside the target
  type.

### D5. Switching: the target-type switch, made one mechanism

`set_calibration_record <id>` is offered in Shutdown and when Homed. It is refused while homing,
parking, faulted (HOME recovers first) or during a run.

1. **Gate.** Before anything changes, the record is read and checked:
   - hashes and schema;
   - the servo limits that `mixed_hardware_profile` enforces: yaw cap ≤ 3 A, RMS ≤ 1.62 A,
     100 RPM, and the pitch guard.

   A bad record is refused with its reason, and the turret is not touched.
2. **In Shutdown:** persist the choice, and configure the disengaged servos and the geometry. The
   next HOME uses it. Nothing moves, so there is no hold.
3. **When Homed:** one *swap hold*, shared by the target-type and record switches. It is today's
   target-type switch, generalised:
   - **Hold.** Remember the mode to resume (AUTO_TRACK resumes as its return mode). MANUAL then
     removes the tracker, and the turret brakes and holds energised. Automatic hand-offs are frozen
     and jogs are refused. A mode chosen meanwhile is the one resumed.
   - **STOP MOTION or HOLD during the swap sets the mode to resume to MANUAL.** That is the fix of
     fact 8, which the shared hold keeps for both switches.
   - **Servo swap.** Assets are parsed off the 1 kHz threads, before the swap. Nothing that holds
     is re-initialised:
     - yaw, under `yaw_mutex_`: release the servo, configure the new parameters and crosstalk table,
       and keep the servo's epoch. The next 5 ms tick re-engages it bumpless, with the integral
       seeded from the applied current;
     - pitch, under `pitch_servo_mutex_`: the same. Meanwhile the drive holds speed zero on its own
       encoder.
   - **Geometry swap**, on the control thread:
     - put the extrinsic and the bore offsets into `tracking_cfg_`;
     - re-place the extrinsic in this power-up's frame;
     - rebuild the LOS solver;
     - bump the tracking-configuration revision, so the HUD redraws the bore mark.

     The startup-only rule in `set_tracking_config` becomes "not while a tracker exists", which the
     hold guarantees.
   - **Applied:** the backend reports the new assets engaged on both axes, and the turret has rested
     within the hold band for 0.3 s with them. The remembered mode then resumes.
   - **Overdue at 10 s:** the turret stays held in MANUAL, and the chip says NOT APPLIED and why.
     Choosing a record again retries. This is not a fault (ruling 2026-10-02).
   - **Both switches at once** share one hold. It ends when every pending swap is applied.
4. **What a swap loses:** the yaw servo's learned friction map and its thermal RMS start again, as
   at every controld start today.
5. **A record that does not match the mounted payload is not detected by the switch.** The station
   cannot see the payload. If the new servo misbehaves, the existing guards decide: an oscillation
   is DEGRADE, and becomes HOLD after its persistence time. VERIFY (D8) is the check.

### D6. The page: MENU > CALIBRATION

Settings live in MENU (ruling 2026-10-03), and a record changes rarely. The section sits under
SETTINGS and is built like the FOLLOW chips:

```
CALIBRATION
 [#1 AS SHIPPED] [#2 RANGEFINDER] [#3 LIGHT CAM]       the record in force is lit; progress bar while switching
 #2 RANGEFINDER   SERVO MEASURED 10-25 · ACCURACY NONE · EXTRINSIC FROM #1 · BORE TRIMMED
 RENAME   VERIFY ~2 MIN   RE-RUN ~45 MIN   NEW...
 BORE  WIDE    RIGHT - +   UP - +    +1.2 / -0.4 MOA   [FINE|COARSE]
       DETAIL  RIGHT - +   UP - +    +0.0 / +0.0 MOA
```

- **Parts** read MEASURED, INHERITED FROM #k, SHIPPED, TRIMMED or NONE, so the page shows what the
  record really knows.
- **RENAME** opens a small dialog outside the drawer. The drawer's repaint rewrites its innerHTML,
  so an input inside it would be wiped.
- **RE-RUN, NEW and VERIFY ask twice and give the duration.** They move the turret through full
  turns at up to 65°/s, for up to an hour, and the confirmation says to keep clear.

### D7. Running a calibration on the station

**What a run measures.** The existing commissioning, with its rules unchanged:

| Action | Steps | Station time (from workstation runs) |
|---|---|---|
| RE-RUN | `pitch`, then `yaw --update` (crosstalk table kept) | ~45 min |
| RE-RUN + ACCURACY | then `calibrate yaw` | ~+25 min |
| NEW | create the record (parts inherited from the chosen record), make it active (D5), then `pitch`, `yaw --update`, `calibrate yaw` | ~70 min |
| VERIFY | `check yaw` (D8) | ~2 min |

- **ACCURACY is off by default for RE-RUN.** The 2026-10-02 ruling re-measures the limits only
  after a deliberate hardware change, and a new payload (NEW) is one.
- **RE-MEASURE CROSSTALK** (`yaw` instead of `yaw --update`) is offered for the case where the yaw
  motor was replaced.
- **NEW from Shutdown** creates and activates the record, then asks for HOME before the run.

**Handing over the motors.** One process drives the motors: the one holding the motion lock. The run
keeps that rule instead of exempting itself from it.

1. **Preconditions.** The run is offered only when Homed and with the IMU fresh. Every session needs
   the IMU, and the stuck-I2C1 incidents of 10-20 and 10-24 would otherwise abort a run. Pressing it
   drops the target into the MANUAL hold.
2. **Run request.** controld writes the request: the record, the steps, and this power-up's pitch
   working window from its own homing. That window replaces the hand-copied one in
   `pitch_prior.json`, which this power-up's frame has already moved away from.
3. **Lend.** controld moves pitch to the centre of the window and stops both axes as a commissioning
   session ends (yaw at zero current, pitch STOP). It then exits with a *lend* code. This is not a
   process stop: SHUTDOWN's rest-stop park would put pitch outside the window.
4. **Launcher.** The launcher holds the motion lock. On the lend code it stops its observe-only IMU
   reader and runs the orchestrator as its child, passing the lock down. webd and visiond keep
   running, so the video stays live.
5. **Orchestrator.** It is `commission.py` with a local station backend in place of ssh/scp and the
   per-run deploy:
   - sessions run through this release's `run_application.sh run --control-yaw|--establish-homing`,
     with this release's `commissiond`, `imu-bno085` and `servo-sim`;
   - output goes to `records/<id>/runs/<run id>/`;
   - after every step it writes `run.json`: step i of n, its name, elapsed and expected time, and
     the last log line.
6. **The page during the run.** With controld away, webd shows the CALIBRATING view from `run.json`.
   ABORT writes an abort request, and the orchestrator stops at the end of the current session,
   which ends the way every session ends.
7. **Result.** On acceptance the orchestrator installs the new parts into the record, moves the
   replaced parts to `history/`, and marks the new ones MEASURED with the run id. A failed gate, an
   abort or a hard abort installs nothing: the record stays as it was, and the report says why.
8. **Return.** The launcher restarts the IMU reader and controld. controld returns the station to the
   state it found, as a deploy does (`tools/station_state.py`): a Homed station homes again, because
   the run moved both axes, and then roams.

**Classification** (rule 1 of the fault/hold ruling). A run is a bounded commissioning session. Its
sessions keep their own quick hard aborts (rule 5), which stay out of production. A failed run is
not a station fault: the record is unchanged and the station returns to where it was.

### D8. VERIFY: does this record fit what is mounted?

VERIFY runs `check yaw` through the same hand-over: one probe of about a minute, scored against the
record's asset, giving `REUSE_EXACT` or `UPDATE_PARAMETERS`. It answers the question the switch
cannot (D5.5).

Two weaknesses must be fixed before it is offered:

- the yaw check ignores the session's end status;
- the pitch gate always passes (`score.py:119-120`).

### D9. Manual adjustment: bore trims

- **Command.** `trim_bore <wide|detail> <right|up> <steps>`. One step is one pixel of that camera at
  the frame centre, and COARSE is ten. The value is stored in degrees (`right_deg`, `up_deg`) in
  the record's `bore.yaml`. The readout is in MOA, the unit of the precision goal.
- **In MANUAL or Shutdown only.**
  - In MANUAL there is no tracker. The HUD's bore mark, recomputed from `tracking_cfg_` at every
    publish, moves at once, and the next automatic mode builds its tracker with the trim.
  - Elsewhere the trim is refused with "trim in MANUAL".
- **The bore mark is drawn only with `alignment.mode: manual_depth`.** The trim rows are offered
  when it is set, and say why when it is not.
- **Persistence** is the same as for the watch point (a writer thread, latest value wins). There is
  one history entry per trimming session: when the section closes, or after 30 s idle.
- **Servo numbers stay read-only, and so does the extrinsic.** Trimming the camera→pitch rotation
  would change every detection's world bearing, with no visible target to trim against.

### D10. Interfaces

| Command | Argument | Offered | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| `set_calibration_record` | id | Shutdown, Homed | D5 |
| `rename_calibration_record` | id, name | whenever controld runs | D2 |
| `new_calibration_record` | source id, name | Shutdown, Homed | D7, NEW |
| `run_calibration` | id, steps | Homed | D7 |
| `verify_calibration` | id | Homed | D8 |
| `trim_bore` | camera, axis, steps | MANUAL, Shutdown | D9 |
| abort (webd writes a request beside `run.json`) | — | during a run | D7.6 |

- **Telemetry:**
  - the record in force: `calibration_record` (id, name);
  - the switch: `calibration_switching`, `calibration_progress`, `calibration_overdue`;
  - the list: `calibration_records`, with id, name, each part's origin and the last run's verdict.
    It is small and changes rarely.
- **webd's protocol model must declare every new field.** It drops undeclared keys.

### D11. Not in this ADR

- **An extrinsic refit as a run step.** No fit code exists (fact 7). A new record inherits the
  extrinsic from its source record, which is right whenever the camera stays where it was on the
  arm. If a payload change moves the camera, the refit is the next piece of work: a fit for
  `stationary_trial.py`, then a run step that needs a stationary object in view.
- **Retiring the v1 payload profiles.** Calibration records supersede them in purpose. The dormant
  code stays until someone removes it deliberately.

## 4. Alternatives considered

- **Commissioning inside controld.**
  - For: the page keeps live telemetry, and pitch could stay energised between sessions.
  - Against: it is the largest change, and it brings commissioning's quick aborts into the
    production process.
  - Revisit if risk 1 bites.
- **A separate systemd unit that stops the whole stack for a run.** It is the simplest hand-over,
  but the page goes dark for up to an hour. Rejected: the owner chose a page that stays up.
- **Git as the store.** A web run would not count until a commit and a deploy. Rejected by the
  owner.

## 5. Consequences

- One record is in force, chosen by the operator and shown on the page. The committed calibration
  files stop being production's source.
- **A change of frame convention in code must migrate every record**, not one file. The 2026-10-20
  re-reference of the extrinsic to the homing contact is an example. Each record says which frame
  its extrinsic is in, and such a release ships the migration, keeping history.
- `calibration/manifest.py`, the WP4 qualification gate, reads the active record's `record.json`
  instead of `manifest.yaml`.
- scipy joins the station venv through `requirements-station.txt`. An arm64 wheel for the venv's
  Python still has to be confirmed.
- The SD card holds journals, about 1 GB per yaw run, pruned to the latest accepted run per record.
- **The servo-commissioning card is out of date on three points** (found while writing this ADR):
  - pitch takes about 25 min, not 10;
  - commissioning needs production stopped ("production is not touched" was only true while
    production was off);
  - production already runs the servo assets (the card still says production yaw uses the old
    speed PI).

## 6. Risks and open questions

1. **Pitch unpowered between sessions.**
   - Commissioning already leaves the idle axis unpowered, held by friction ("within one sensor
     count", measured with the 3 kg payload), and the hand-over adds one more such gap.
   - The owner called a released pitch dangerous with a heavy payload (2026-10-03).
   - Before the first heavier payload, establish whether friction holds it. For example, compare
     the pitch holding effort at the window centre with the static friction, and refuse the run
     when they are too close.
   - Longer term, commissiond sessions could hold the idle axis at speed zero on its drive.
2. **Pitch session overhead.** A pitch run takes 25 min where commissiond's own limits add up to
   about 9; the likely cause is the Pi's Python review of the journal. Measure it before the page
   quotes run times.
3. **Memory.** `journal.py` loads whole journals, up to 280 MB, into memory. Check that this fits
   beside visiond.
4. **visiond without controld.** visiond reads controld's context over its socket. Confirm that the
   camera and the video keep running through an absence of up to an hour.
5. **A yaw motor change makes every record's crosstalk table stale**, and the station cannot tell.
   The runbook will say so. RE-MEASURE CROSSTALK fixes the record in use.

## 7. Stages

**Stage 1: records and switching, no runs.**

- Scope:
  - the store, seeding and import, `active.json`, and the D4 boot rule;
  - the shared swap hold with the STOP fix;
  - the servo and geometry hot swap;
  - rename, bore trims, MENU > CALIBRATION and the header line;
  - the pull tool.
- Verification on the station. A real second record already exists: the accepted 2026-10-20
  `yaw --update` candidate (same gains, friction 38-62 % lower;
  `run/servo-commission/update-20261020/yaw_servo.json`), imported as a record.
  - Switch #1 ↔ #2 from AUTO_ROAM, SURVEILLANCE, a track and MANUAL, with yaw and pitch logged
    through the swap. They must stay within the hold band.
  - Time each switch from the tap to the resumed mode.
  - STOP during a switch stays MANUAL.
  - Switch in Shutdown, then HOME.
  - A reboot and a deploy both keep the choice.
  - A corrupted record refuses HOME and gives its reason.

**Stage 2: runs on the station.**

- Scope:
  - the local station backend and scipy;
  - the lend code and the launcher hand-over;
  - `run.json`, the CALIBRATING view and abort;
  - the live pitch window, the IMU precheck and journal pruning;
  - RE-RUN, NEW and VERIFY;
  - a rewritten card.
- Verification: a RE-RUN of the current payload from the page, end to end. Compare its asset with
  the 10-02 one, and time each step on the Pi.

**Stage 3: only when needed.** The extrinsic fit and its run step.

**Unit tests:**

- the store and seeding;
- the D4 refusals and the gate's refusals;
- the swap-hold state machine: applied, overdue, STOP, two switches at once;
- the servo hot swap on the simulated backend: no trip and no step in position;
- the geometry swap: revision bump and re-placement;
- trim bounds and the HUD rows;
- the orchestrator's local backend against `SimStation`.
