# ADR-005: Calibration records — indexed payload sets, chosen from the web

- **Date:** 2026-10-24 (NZDT). Revised the same day after the owner's walkthrough of the web
  mock-up.
- **Status:** Proposed. The decisions in section 2 are the owner's; nothing is implemented.
- **Request (owner, 2026-10-24):** several calibration records, indexed. From the web: select one,
  rename it, re-run the calibration of the current one, add a new one and run it, adjust it by hand.
  A switch follows the target-type switch policy.
- **Builds on:** the target-type switch ([DESIGN_TARGET_TYPE_2026-10-20.md](../DESIGN_TARGET_TYPE_2026-10-20.md)),
  servo commissioning ([card](../operations/servo-commissioning.md)), and the fault/hold, two-state
  and web-page rulings in the [runbook](../STATION_OPERATIONS.md).
- **Mock-up:** `run/mockups/calibration/calibration-panel-proposal.html`, built from the station's own
  `hud.py` page by `run/mockups/calibration/build.py`, with a fake controld in the browser. It is
  local and not in git (`run/` is ignored).

## 1. Context

### What exists today

There is one copy of everything. Each calibration is one file at a fixed path, in git, shipped with
every release:

| Part | File | Read by | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Yaw servo asset | `config/servo/yaw_servo.json` | controld's backend (`load_servos`, `mixed_can_motor_backend.cpp`) | once, at backend open |
| Pitch servo asset | `config/servo/pitch_servo.json` | the same | the same |
| Yaw accuracy limits | `config/servo/yaw_accuracy.json` | commissioning only (`score.py`); no production process reads it | — |
| Camera→pitch extrinsic | `calibration/camera_extrinsics.yaml` | controld (`tracking_setup.hpp`); placed into each power-up's pitch frame after homing | at start; startup-only |
| Bore offsets | `alignment.camera_offset.{wide,detail}` in `config/turret_mixed.yaml` | controld's tracker and the HUD bore mark (via telemetry) | at start; `set_tracking_config` throws if they change while tracking |

A payload change today means running `commission.py` from the workstation, committing the asset and
deploying. Going back to an earlier payload means digging in git or commissioning again. The only
mechanism with several copies is the v1 `config/payload_profiles/` (named profiles,
`payload.active_profile`), which is dormant on the mixed station and unreachable from the page.

### Facts that shape the design (read from the code, 2026-10-24)

1. **Commissioning cannot run beside production.** Both take `/tmp/ota-motion-$UID.lock`
   (`run_application.sh:355`). The session supervisor also refuses while controld or imu-bno085
   exists (`adr0022_capture_launch.py:209-224`). One process drives the CAN buses.
2. **A commissioning session leaves the axis it does not test unpowered**, held only by friction
   (card: "within one sensor count", measured with the 3 kg payload). Each session ends with yaw at
   zero current and pitch STOP.
3. **Everything a run needs already ships to the Pi, except scipy.** Every prebuilt release carries
   arm64 `commissiond`, `servo-sim` and `imu-bno085`. numpy is in `requirements-station.txt`;
   scipy is not.
4. **The workstation's share of a run is small.** The design step takes 10-15 s per yaw run, and
   all the computation about a minute. Station time dominates (workstation-run file times):
   - `yaw --update`: 19 min of sessions;
   - `pitch`: 25 min, not the card's 10; the extra time is outside commissiond and unexplained;
   - `calibrate yaw`: 22 min of sessions plus 4 min of scoring.

   A full yaw run writes about 1 GB of journals.
5. **Reconfiguring an engaged yaw servo trips it.** `Servo::configure` clears its ready flag, and
   the next step latches `servo_data_invalid`. A bumpless path exists, though:
   - `release_yaw_servo_locked` releases the servo;
   - the next 5 ms tick re-engages it through `command_reference`;
   - `Servo::reset` seeds the integral from the applied current.

   Pitch re-engages with its integral at zero, as every engage does today. Meanwhile the drive
   holds speed zero on its own encoder.
6. **The geometry has a natural seam.** In the MANUAL hold there is no tracker. Resuming a mode
   rebuilds the tracker from `tracking_cfg_` (`enable_tracking`). `place_extrinsic_in_session`
   places the extrinsic in the power-up's frame after homing.
7. **There is no code that fits the extrinsic.** The 2026-10-05 rotation of −41.765° was computed
   by hand from `stationary_trial.py` runs. The later re-references to the homed frame were hand
   edits.
8. **The target-type switch is specific to target type** (`TargetSwitch`, `step_target_switch`,
   `control_loop.cpp:4695-4748`). Writing this ADR found a gap in it: STOP MOTION or HOLD during a
   switch did not clear the remembered mode, so the turret resumed AUTO_ROAM when the swap
   completed, against the operator's last command. It was fixed on 2026-10-24, in the commit that
   adds this ADR (test `TargetTypeSwitch.AStopDuringTheSwapIsNotUndoneWhenItEnds`).
9. **The page shows the D-pad in MANUAL even while controld refuses jogs**, as it does during a swap
   hold. The mock-up showed this for the target-type switch too.

## 2. Owner decisions (2026-10-24)

Before the walkthrough:

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| What a record holds | A **payload set**: everything that changes when what sits on the pitch arm changes. |
| Running a calibration | **On the station**, from the web button. The page stays up. |
| Manual adjustment | **Aim trims only**: the bore offsets. Servo numbers are read-only. |
| Storage | **A station store** in `run/state/calibration/`, which survives deploys. Today's committed files become record #1. A tool copies records back into the repository. |
| Switching | The target-type policy: drop the target, hold energised, swap, resume. Overdue at 10 s → HOLD, no fault. |

In the walkthrough of the mock-up:

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| Where on the page | **MENU › CALIBRATION** opens a dedicated panel. Calibration is an internal setting that rarely changes, so nothing about it sits in the mode block. |
| MANUAL | **The MANUAL dock button and drawer go.** The D-pad is the manual control. |
| Removing a record | **Records leave the way they came**, from the page. **#1 AS SHIPPED is the failsafe backup** and is never removed. |
| A deploy's calibration files | **No hash check against the source code**: it makes no sense and would make every future update hard. The committed files seed #1 once, on an empty store; a deploy never compares them with the records again. New calibration from the workstation arrives by an explicit import. |
| After a switch while Homed | **Resume the interrupted mode**, exactly as the HUMAN/HEAD/CAT/DOG switch does. |
| VERIFY after a switch | **A nudge, never automatic.** |
| Zeroing distance | **Set with the trim.** For a laser device the camera's offset means a configurable zeroing distance; until now infinity was assumed. |
| Trim step | **MRAD clicks.** |
| Calibration state | **A run puts the station into CALIBRATING**, where no manual or automatic control intervenes. This is new in this ADR. |
| After a run | **The mode it interrupted.** |
| The axis not under test | **Held while the other is commissioned**, so runs do not vary. |
| Who runs the sessions | **controld**, which keeps the motors for the whole run. |
| Page fixes found in the mock-up | **Dim the D-pad during swap holds**, and **widen the drawer on phones**. |

## 3. Decisions

### D1. A record is a payload set

| Part | Why it belongs to the payload |
|---|---|
| Yaw servo asset | inertia, friction, and the gains designed from them |
| Pitch servo asset | the speed loop's gain under this load, and the gains |
| Yaw accuracy limits | measured with this payload's servo |
| Camera→pitch extrinsic | changes when the camera's mounting on the arm changes |
| Bore (wide, detail): mm offsets, trims, zeroing distance | where each camera looks relative to the payload's point of aim |

These stay shared, because they are station facts, not payload facts:

- lens intrinsics;
- the camera mount orientation (`camera_install.yaml`);
- homing contacts (retained per power-up) and the pitch working window from homing;
- the installation pose;
- the watch point, the target type and the live speeds.

The yaw asset keeps its encoder crosstalk table, although the table is a property of the motor, not
the payload. Splitting it out would break the asset's single provenance. The consequence is in
risk 4.

### D2. Index, name and life cycle

- **Id.** An integer assigned at creation, never reused, shown as `#N` (as tracks are). The id is
  the identity: commands and the persisted choice use it.
- **Name.** A label of up to 24 characters (letters, digits, space, `-`, `_`), shown upper case.
  It can be edited at any time, and two records may share a name.
- **#1 AS SHIPPED is the failsafe backup.** It is seeded from the release's committed files and
  is locked:
  - never removed, renamed, measured into or trimmed;
  - with #1 in force, RE-RUN reads **MEASURE A COPY…** (NEW from #1), and the trims say why
    they are locked;
  - VERIFY is allowed: it installs nothing, and its result goes into the run history, not the
    parts.
- **Removal.** The panel's REMOVE row takes two presses and offers every record except #1 and the
  record in force. The station moves a removed record to `deleted/` instead of erasing it, because
  each measured record holds an hour of station time. The store tool purges.

### D3. Storage: the station store

```
run/state/calibration/
  active.json                   {"schema": 1, "active": 2}
  records/0002/record.json      name, parts, provenance
  records/0002/yaw_servo.json  pitch_servo.json  yaw_accuracy.json  camera_extrinsics.yaml  bore.yaml
  records/0002/history/<time>/  every part that a run or a trim replaced
  records/0002/runs/<run id>/   commission.log, report.json, report.md, session journals
  incoming/<name>/              a record imported from the workstation, adopted at controld's next start
  deleted/0003/                 a removed record, whole
```

- **Location.** `deploy_station.py` already links `run/state` into every release. It survives
  deploys and reboots, as `watch_point.json` and `target_type.json` do.
- **The directory name is the id.** `record.json` holds the name and each part's file and origin:
  - `measured`: run id and date;
  - `inherited`: from record #k;
  - `trimmed`;
  - `shipped`: git revision.

  It also carries the frame its extrinsic is in (`pitch_from_homed_low`).
- **No hashes** (owner, 2026-10-24). A record is usable when its files parse and the servo gate
  (D5) accepts its assets. Editing a record's file by hand, as the extrinsic was re-referenced on
  10-06 and 10-20, needs no bookkeeping.
- **`bore.yaml`** holds the bore mode, the zeroing distance and, per camera, the mm offsets, the
  base angles and the trims in mrad (D9).
- **Writes are atomic** (temp file, then rename), and controld is the only writer of `records/`
  and `active.json`. Tools write only into `incoming/`.
- **What the station keeps.** It never deletes history. It keeps the journals of the latest
  accepted run per record, because `calibrate yaw` needs the asset's own validation passes. Older
  journals are pruned and their reports kept.

**Seeding.** At start, before controld, the launcher runs `tools/calibration_records.py seed`.
- **An empty store** gets record #1, `AS SHIPPED`, from the release's committed files, and it
  becomes active. Those files are the servo assets, the accuracy limits, the extrinsic and the
  alignment block.
- **A store with records is left alone.** A deploy never compares the committed files with the
  records, so changing a calibration file in git creates nothing on the station.

**Import.** `tools/calibration_records.py import --name NAME` stages a record in `incoming/`.
- Each part is a file given on the command line, or is inherited from a named record.
- controld adopts it at its next start, under the next free id, and it is never activated by
  itself.
- This is how a workstation commissioning result reaches the station in stage 1, and how the
  accepted 2026-10-20 candidate becomes record #2 for the stage 1 verification.

**Back to the repository.** `tools/calibration_records.py pull`, run on the workstation, copies
records without their journals into `Firmware/calibration/records/<id>-<name>/`, where they are
committed as evidence. Journals stay runtime captures, outside git.

### D4. Which record is in force

- controld reads `active.json` at start. It loads that record's parts before it builds the tracking
  configuration and opens the backend (`main.cpp`, around lines 209 and 269).
- **An active record that is missing or unusable** brings controld up in Shutdown with no servo
  configured. A record is unusable when a file does not parse, or the gate refuses a servo asset.
  HOME is refused with the reason, for example "record #2: yaw_servo.json does not parse; choose a
  record".
  - controld never falls back to another record by itself. A servo designed for another payload is
    the very failure this feature exists to prevent.
  - Boot is Shutdown, so nothing moves, and this is not a fault.
- **The page shows the record in force in two places**: the MENU row that opens the panel
  (`CALIBRATION › #2 FRICTION 10-20`) and the panel itself. Nothing is added to the mode block.

### D5. Switching: the target-type switch, made one mechanism

`set_calibration_record <id>` is offered in Shutdown and when Homed. It is refused while homing,
parking, faulted (HOME recovers first) or calibrating.

1. **Gate.** Before anything changes, the record is read and checked:
   - every part's file parses;
   - the servo limits that `mixed_hardware_profile` enforces: yaw cap ≤ 3 A, RMS ≤ 1.62 A,
     100 RPM, and the pitch guard.

   A bad record is refused with its reason, and the turret is not touched.
2. **In Shutdown:** persist the choice, and configure the disengaged servos and the geometry. The
   next HOME uses it. Nothing moves, so there is no hold. This is the normal payload change: motors
   off, change the payload, choose its record, HOME.
3. **When Homed:** one *swap hold*, shared by the target-type and record switches. It is today's
   target-type switch, generalised:
   - **Hold.** Remember the mode to resume (AUTO_TRACK resumes as its return mode). MANUAL then
     removes the tracker, and the turret brakes and holds energised. Automatic hand-offs are frozen
     and jogs are refused. A mode chosen meanwhile is the one resumed.
   - **The page dims the D-pad during any swap hold**, the target-type switch included, because
     controld refuses jogs then (fact 9).
   - **STOP MOTION or HOLD during the swap sets the mode to resume to MANUAL.** That is the fix of
     fact 8, which the shared hold keeps for both switches.
   - **Servo swap.** Assets are parsed off the 1 kHz threads, before the swap. Nothing that holds
     is re-initialised:
     - yaw, under `yaw_mutex_`: release the servo, configure the new parameters and crosstalk table,
       and keep the servo's epoch. The next 5 ms tick re-engages it bumpless, with the integral
       seeded from the applied current;
     - pitch, under `pitch_servo_mutex_`: the same. Meanwhile the drive holds speed zero on its own
       encoder.
   - **Geometry swap**, on the control thread:
     - put the extrinsic and the bore into `tracking_cfg_`;
     - re-place the extrinsic in this power-up's frame;
     - rebuild the LOS solver;
     - bump the tracking-configuration revision, so the HUD redraws the bore mark.

     The startup-only rule in `set_tracking_config` becomes "not while a tracker exists", which the
     hold guarantees.
   - **Applied:** the backend reports the new assets engaged on both axes, and the turret has rested
     within the hold band for 0.3 s with them. The interrupted mode then resumes, exactly as after a
     target-type switch.
   - **Overdue at 10 s:** the turret stays held in MANUAL, and the chip says NOT APPLIED and why.
     Choosing a record again retries. This is not a fault (ruling 2026-10-02).
   - **Both switches at once** share one hold. It ends when every pending swap is applied.
4. **What a swap loses:** the yaw servo's learned friction map and its thermal RMS start again, as
   at every controld start today.
5. **The switch cannot tell whether the record fits the mounted payload.** The station cannot see
   the payload. If the new servo misbehaves, the existing guards decide: an oscillation is DEGRADE,
   and becomes HOLD after its persistence time.
   - **A switch sets `calibration_unverified`**, and the panel's VERIFY row then reads NOT VERIFIED
     SINCE SWITCH.
   - A VERIFY, or an accepted measurement, clears it.
   - **A run never starts by itself**: it turns the turret at up to 65°/s.

### D6. The page: MENU › CALIBRATION, a dedicated panel

- **Entry.** The first row of MENU › SETTINGS, `CALIBRATION › #N NAME`, which is also where the page
  shows the record in force. It opens a panel in the drawer's place (one drawer at a time), with
  MENU still lit in the dock. The panel can be reached from every mode and from Shutdown.
- **The panel, top to bottom:**
  1. `‹ MENU` back;
  2. `IN FORCE #N NAME` with RENAME, or `SHIPPED BACKUP · LOCKED` for #1;
  3. the record chips, drawn like the FOLLOW chips. The record in force is lit and inert, an unusable
     record is amber with `!`, and a switch shows the same progress bar as the target type;
  4. **WHAT #N KNOWS:** YAW SERVO, PITCH SERVO, ACCURACY, EXTRINSIC, BORE, each MEASURED date,
     SHIPPED, FROM #k, TRIMMED or NONE, plus LAST RUN. A record whose servo parts are all inherited
     says NOT MEASURED;
  5. **MEASURE:** VERIFY, RE-RUN with +ACCURACY and +CROSSTALK (MEASURE A COPY… on #1), NEW…,
     REMOVE;
  6. **BORE TRIM** (D9);
  7. STOP MOTION.
- **Dialogs** sit outside the drawer, because the drawer's repaint rewrites its innerHTML:
  - RENAME;
  - NEW: name, the record to start from, what to measure, the duration, and a keep-clear warning.
- **Two presses** for VERIFY, RE-RUN, NEW's start and REMOVE. One press for STOP MOTION and STOP.
- **The MANUAL dock button and drawer are removed.** The D-pad, shown in MANUAL, is the manual
  control. The four exact ±1° step rows go with the drawer; `manual_step` stays in the API for the
  tools that use it (`stationary_trial.py`).
- **Phones.** Below 600 px the drawer and the panel are `min(330px, 92vw)` and `min(440px, 92vw)`
  wide. Today's `32vw` is about 120 px on a phone, already too narrow for MENU's own rows.

### D7. Running a calibration: CALIBRATING, inside controld

**What a run measures.** The existing commissioning, with its rules unchanged:

| Action | Steps | Station time (from workstation runs) |
|---|---|---|
| VERIFY | `check yaw` (D8) | ~2 min |
| RE-RUN | `pitch`, then `yaw --update` (crosstalk table kept) | ~45 min |
| RE-RUN + ACCURACY | then `calibrate yaw` | ~+26 min |
| RE-RUN + CROSSTALK | `yaw` instead of `yaw --update`: after a yaw motor change | ~+2 min |
| NEW | create the record (parts inherited from the chosen one), switch to it (D5), then `pitch`, `yaw --update`, `calibrate yaw` | ~71 min |

- **ACCURACY is off by default for RE-RUN.** The 2026-10-02 ruling re-measures the limits only
  after a deliberate hardware change, and a new payload (NEW) is one.
- **NEW from Shutdown** creates the record and puts it in force, then asks for HOME before the run.
- **On #1** RE-RUN is MEASURE A COPY…, which is NEW from #1.

**CALIBRATING.** It is a controld phase like HOMING, not a fifth operating mode: MODE does not offer
it, and it ends by itself.

- VERIFY, RE-RUN and NEW's measurement enter it. Swap holds and trims do not: zeroing needs the
  D-pad to aim.
- **Nothing manual or automatic acts.** controld refuses mode changes, jogs, target selection and
  automatic hand-offs, with "calibrating: no manual or automatic control until the run ends; STOP
  ends it now".
- **The page** shows the mode block as `CALIBRATING` / `<kind> · <step>`. It dims the dock, hides
  the D-pad, and replaces the drawer with the run view:
  - the steps, with the current session and the elapsed and expected times;
  - the last log line;
  - STOP.
- **STOP takes one press and acts now.** It ends the current session at once, leaves both axes
  held, abandons the run and installs nothing. It does not wait for the session to end, because
  an abandoned run installs nothing anyway.
- **Offered only when Homed**, with the IMU fresh. Every session needs the IMU, and the stuck-I2C1
  incidents of 10-20 and 10-24 would otherwise abort a run.

**The sessions run inside controld.** The owner's ruling that the axis not under test is held, so
runs do not vary, rules out handing the motors to `commissiond` (facts 1-2: a released idle axis in
every session, and a process switch at each end).

1. **controld keeps the motors and the motion lock for the whole run.** The axis under test follows
   the session's script. The other is held by production's own hold: yaw by its servo at its
   position, pitch by its speed loop at the window centre.
2. **The session executors move into a library that controld links.** These are commissiond's yaw
   sessions (survey, crosstalk, inertia, ladder, circle, use case, probe) and its pitch sessions
   (identify, ladder, use case).
   - They run only in CALIBRATING.
   - Their quick hard aborts stay confined to that phase (fault/hold rule 5), and an abort ends the
     session with both axes held, not released.
3. **The orchestrator is `commission.py` as a stack process, `calibd`.** The launcher starts it
   beside webd and visiond, and it is idle until a run. During a run it:
   - sends controld one session manifest at a time;
   - reads the journal controld writes, in the format `journal.py` already parses;
   - fits and designs between sessions while controld holds;
   - asks controld to install the accepted parts.
4. **Journals are written off the real-time thread.** Capture keeps the kernel receive timestamps
   (`TimestampedReceiver`) that identification depends on.
5. **The pitch working window comes from controld's own homing**, not from the hand-copied window
   in `pitch_prior.json`, which this power-up's frame has already moved away from.
6. **At the end controld installs the accepted parts** into the record and moves the replaced ones
   to `history/`. It then HOMEs, because the run moved both axes, and resumes the mode the run
   interrupted.
7. **The page stays live throughout.** controld publishes `calibration_run`, so nothing on the page
   has to handle an absent controller.

**Classification** (rule 1 of the fault/hold ruling). A run is a bounded commissioning activity. A
failed or stopped run is not a station fault: the record is unchanged, and the station returns to
the mode it interrupted.

### D8. VERIFY: does this record fit what is mounted?

VERIFY runs `check yaw` in CALIBRATING: one probe of about a minute, scored against the record's
asset, giving `REUSE_EXACT` or `UPDATE_PARAMETERS`. It answers the question the switch cannot
(D5.5). It is offered on every record, #1 included.

Two weaknesses must be fixed before it is offered:

- the yaw check ignores the session's end status;
- the pitch gate always passes (`score.py:119-120`).

### D9. Manual adjustment: bore trims and the zeroing distance

- **Commands.** `trim_bore <wide|detail> <right|up> <mrad>` and `set_bore_zero <m|inf>`.
- **MRAD clicks.** Three paces: 0.1 (the MIL click), 1 and 10 mrad. The readout is in MRAD, with
  pixels beside it.
  - On the wide camera 0.1 mrad is 0.14 px and 1 mrad is 1.4 px.
  - 10 mrad is for a first large correction: a mount 0.5° off is 8.7 mrad.
  - Steps finer than a pixel become visible on the detail camera once its intrinsics are measured.
- **The zeroing distance is part of the zero.** ZERO AT steps through 5, 10, 25, 50, 100, 200, 500,
  1000, 1500 m and ∞, and is saved with the trim.
  - The camera's mm offset from the bore gives a parallax of offset / distance: 7.5 mrad at 10 m,
    0.05 mrad at 1500 m, none at ∞.
  - Zero on a target at the chosen distance. A zero made at one distance while another is assumed
    absorbs the parallax error and is wrong everywhere else.
  - The HUD labels the bore mark `ZERO 25 m` or `ZERO ∞`.
  - A rangefinder later supplies the distance.
- **When.** Only in MANUAL hold or Shutdown, with the turret still. In AUTO modes the panel offers
  HOLD HERE TO TRIM, which is MODE › MANUAL in one press. #1 is locked.
- **BORE ON/OFF is per record** (owner, 2026-10-24), `set_bore_mode <on|off>`. ON, the tracker aims
  the bore instead of the camera centre and the HUD draws the amber bore mark; OFF, it aims the
  camera centre. ZERO AT and the trims need ON. #1 keeps the shipped `off` and is locked; a copy
  can turn it on.
- **The detail rows wait for the detail camera's intrinsics:** its field of view is not measured.
- **Persistence** is the same as for the watch point (a writer thread, latest value wins), with one
  history entry per trimming session.
- **Servo numbers and the extrinsic stay read-only.** Trimming the camera→pitch rotation would
  change every detection's world bearing, with no visible target to trim against.

### D10. Interfaces

| Command | Argument | Offered | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| `set_calibration_record` | id | Shutdown, Homed | D5 |
| `rename_calibration_record` | id, name | any time, not #1 | D2 |
| `new_calibration_record` | source id, name, what to measure | Shutdown, Homed | D7, NEW |
| `delete_calibration_record` | id | not #1, not the record in force | D2 |
| `run_calibration` | id, options | Homed, not #1 | D7 |
| `verify_calibration` | id | Homed | D8 |
| `stop_calibration` | — | CALIBRATING | D7, STOP |
| `set_bore_mode` | `on` or `off` | MANUAL, Shutdown; not #1 | D9 |
| `trim_bore` | camera, axis, mrad | MANUAL, Shutdown; not #1; bore on | D9 |
| `set_bore_zero` | metres or `inf` | MANUAL, Shutdown; not #1; bore on | D9 |

- **Telemetry:**
  - the record in force: `calibration_record` (id, name);
  - the switch: `calibration_switching`, `calibration_progress`, `calibration_overdue`,
    `calibration_unverified`;
  - the list: `calibration_records`, with id, name, each part's origin, the trims and the last
    run's verdict. It is small and changes rarely;
  - a run: `calibration_run`, with the steps, the current session, the elapsed and expected times,
    the last log line and the verdict;
  - the bore: `alignment.zero_m`, beside the existing alignment fields.
- **webd's protocol model must declare every new field.** It drops undeclared keys.

### D11. Not in this ADR

- **An extrinsic refit as a run step.** No fit code exists (fact 7). A new record inherits the
  extrinsic from its source record, which is right whenever the camera stays where it was on the
  arm. If a payload change moves the camera, the refit is the next piece of work: a fit for
  `stationary_trial.py`, then a run step that needs a stationary object in view.
- **Retiring the v1 payload profiles.** Calibration records supersede them in purpose. The dormant
  code stays until someone removes it deliberately.

## 4. Alternatives considered

- **Lend the motors to commissiond** (this ADR's first version: controld exits, the launcher runs
  the sessions, controld returns). Rejected on 2026-10-24: every session and both hand-overs leave
  the idle axis unheld, so runs would vary. It also needed a lend exit code, a launcher hand-over
  and a page view for an absent controller.
- **One commissiond process per run, holding both axes.** It closes the gaps between sessions but
  not the two process switches, and it rebuilds controld's hold inside commissiond.
- **A separate systemd unit that stops the whole stack for a run.** The page would go dark for up to
  an hour. Rejected: the page stays up.
- **Git as the store.** A web run would not count until a commit and a deploy. Rejected by the
  owner.
- **A CALIBRATION section in MENU, or an entry in the MANUAL drawer** (the first two mock-ups).
  Replaced by the dedicated panel opened from MENU.

## 5. Consequences

- One record is in force, chosen by the operator and shown in MENU. The committed calibration files
  stop being production's source.
- **A change of frame convention in code must migrate every record**, not one file. The 2026-10-20
  re-reference of the extrinsic to the homing contact is an example. Each record says which frame
  its extrinsic is in, and such a release ships the migration, keeping history.
- `calibration/manifest.py`, the WP4 qualification gate, reads the active record's `record.json`
  instead of `manifest.yaml`, and drops its hash comparison.
- scipy joins the station venv through `requirements-station.txt`. An arm64 wheel for the venv's
  Python still has to be confirmed.
- The SD card holds journals, about 1 GB per yaw run, pruned to the latest accepted run per record.
- The D-pad dimming changes the target-type switch's page too.
- **The servo-commissioning card is out of date on three points** (found while writing this ADR):
  - pitch takes about 25 min, not 10;
  - commissioning needs production stopped ("production is not touched" was only true while
    production was off);
  - production already runs the servo assets (the card still says production yaw uses the old
    speed PI).

  Stage 2 rewrites it: on the station, commissioning is a run from the page, and `commission.py`
  over ssh stays for development.

## 6. Risks and open questions

1. **Moving the session executors into controld** is the largest stage-2 item.
   - The real-time structure must keep the 1 kHz servo threads clean.
   - The 1 kHz journals (about 1 GB per yaw run) must be written off the real-time thread.
   - The kernel receive timestamps must survive.

   First move one session (pitch identify) and compare its journal and fit with commissiond's for
   the same plant.
2. **Pitch session overhead.** A pitch run takes 25 min where commissiond's own limits add up to
   about 9; the likely cause is the Pi's Python review of the journal around each session. It may
   disappear once there is no per-session launcher. Measure it before the page quotes run times.
3. **Memory.** `journal.py` loads whole journals, up to 280 MB, into memory. Check that this fits
   beside visiond.
4. **A yaw motor change makes every record's crosstalk table stale**, and the station cannot tell.
   The runbook will say so. RE-RUN + CROSSTALK fixes the record in use.
5. **The CyberGear's behaviour when the host goes silent** (its CAN timeout) now matters only for
   controld's own restarts, not for runs. Check it with stage 1.

## 7. Stages

**Stage 1: records and switching on the page, no runs.**

- Scope:
  - the store, seeding and import, `active.json`, and the D4 boot rule;
  - the shared swap hold (its STOP fix is already in, `e9e6f5f`) and the D-pad dimming;
  - the servo and geometry hot swap;
  - MENU › CALIBRATION and the panel, and the removal of the MANUAL dock button and drawer;
  - rename, REMOVE, and #1's lock;
  - bore trims in MRAD with the zeroing distance, and `alignment.mode` on;
  - the drawer width on phones;
  - the pull tool.
- Verification on the station. A real second record already exists: the accepted 2026-10-20
  `yaw --update` candidate (same gains, friction 38-62 % lower;
  `run/servo-commission/update-20261020/yaw_servo.json`), imported as a record.
  - Switch #1 ↔ #2 from AUTO_ROAM, SURVEILLANCE, a track and MANUAL, with yaw and pitch logged
    through the swap. They must stay within the hold band.
  - Time each switch from the tap to the resumed mode.
  - STOP during a switch stays MANUAL.
  - Switch in Shutdown, then HOME.
  - A reboot and a deploy both keep the choice.
  - A corrupted record refuses HOME and gives its reason.
  - #1 refuses rename, removal and trims.
  - A zero made at a measured distance lands the bore mark on the target there.

**Stage 2: runs inside controld.**

- Scope:
  - the session-executor library in controld, the CALIBRATING phase and STOP;
  - `calibd` and scipy;
  - the journals, the live pitch window, the IMU precheck and journal pruning;
  - VERIFY, RE-RUN and NEW;
  - a rewritten card.
- Verification:
  - a RE-RUN of the current payload from the page, end to end;
  - the idle axis logged through every session: it must stay within one sensor count;
  - its asset compared with the 10-02 one;
  - each step timed on the Pi;
  - STOP pressed mid-session leaves both axes held.

**Stage 3: only when needed.** The extrinsic fit and its run step.

**Unit tests:**

- the store, seeding and removal, and #1's lock;
- the D4 refusals and the gate's refusals;
- the swap-hold state machine: applied, overdue, STOP, two switches at once, the unverified flag;
- the servo hot swap on the simulated backend: no trip and no step in position;
- the geometry swap: revision bump, re-placement, the zeroing distance's parallax;
