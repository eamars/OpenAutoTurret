# ADR-006: Camera zero — both cameras on the payload's line of sight

- **Date:** 2026-10-24 (NZDT).
- **Status:** Draft, for the owner's grilling. Nothing is implemented. The video feed stays closed
  to the agent until the owner opens it for the measurement (section 6).
- **Request (owner, 2026-10-24):** a calibration routine for the camera offsets, done visually:
  adjust each camera's crosshair so that the tracking point agrees with the payload's line of sight.
  "Both cameras have an offset to the payload. Today the installation offset is recorded, but the
  point-of-aim offset at a known distance is not. The installation offset plus a crosshair
  calibration of both cameras should give the full camera calibration."
- **Builds on:** ADR-005 D9 (bore trims, the zeroing distance, BORE ON/OFF per record), the bore
  model in `control/src/geometry/bore_alignment.hpp`, and ADR-001's main display (only the camera
  on the main display feeds the detector; the detail picture is published in the wide frame).

## 1. Context

### What exists today (read from the code, 2026-10-24)

1. **One model, two cameras.** `geo::CameraOffset` gives each camera its *installation* position
   relative to the bore (`right_mm`, `up_mm`, `forward_mm`) and its *pointing* relative to the bore
   (`right_deg`, `up_deg`). `geo::bore_alignment` projects the bore's point at the assumed depth
   into a camera exactly: rotation Ry(right)·Rx(up), no small-angle shortcut. That gives the amber
   crosshair (the "bore mark") for the camera on the main display.
2. **The detail camera lives in the wide frame.** Its picture is a window 1/5.90 of the wide frame,
   centred (`perception/detection/view.py`). 5.90 was measured on 2026-10-02 by
   `tools/register_detail_view.py` (NCC 0.60, roll between the cameras within 0.5°). In that
   "virtual wide" frame the wide intrinsics serve both cameras, because the focal-length ratio
   cancels.
3. **The numbers are placeholders.** `config/turret_mixed.yaml` and every record's `bore.yaml` say
   75 mm right, 75 mm up, 0 forward, pointing 0°, for both cameras, marked "UNMEASURED: parallel
   axes assumed, and the detail camera given the wide camera's position".
4. **Only the wide crosshair can be adjusted.** ADR-005 D9 gives MRAD trims and a ZERO AT ladder
   (5 m to ∞) for the wide camera. The detail rows say "needs the detail camera's intrinsics".
5. **The tracker always reasons as the wide camera.** It builds the solver and the estimator from
   the wide offset (`control_loop.cpp` `enable_tracking`, the LOS intent's `sight_camera`). A frame
   from the detail camera arrives flagged (`obs.narrow_view`, wire flag bit 1), but its boxes are
   treated as wide-camera rays. So whatever separates the two cameras' pointing becomes an error in
   the target's world bearing, and a step at every camera swap. The 2026-10-02 registration put the
   detail view at 0.49/0.45 of the wide frame for a subject at about 1 m, about 1°.
6. **The tracker's depth is the zeroing distance.** With BORE ON, `alignment.assumed_depth_m` is the
   record's ZERO AT value; there is no range sensor yet.
7. **Resolution.** Wide: fx 1389 px at 1920 (theodolite session 2026-09-04), 0.72 mrad per pixel.
   Detail: about 0.12 mrad per pixel (5.90×).
8. **The turret settles inside a hold band.** Yaw settles 0.26–0.35° from where it was sent (the
   servo's 4 mrad hold band); pitch holds 0.2 mrad rms. A payload pointed with the D-pad can settle
   a few mrad off the target.

### What is missing

The point-of-aim offset: where each camera actually points relative to the payload. Every
crosshair today is drawn from the placeholder angles, so it is only right by accident.

## 3. Decisions (proposed)

### D1. A zero measures each camera's pointing, given its installation position

The payload points at a target T at distance D along its line of sight: T = (0, 0, D) in the
bore's frame. Camera c sits at its installation position t_c and sees T at the picture point the
operator taps, a ray r in its own frame. Its pointing R_c = Ry(right)·Rx(up) is the rotation that
turns r onto the direction from the camera to the target:

  R_c · r̂ = (T − t_c) / |T − t_c|

Two angles, two equations, solved exactly (Rx fixes the vertical component, then Ry the
horizontal). This is the inverse of `bore_alignment`, so a solved zero puts that camera's crosshair
exactly on the tapped point at distance D, by construction.

- **Installation offset (t_c):** entered, not measured by the routine (owner: it is recorded). The
  owner's numbers (section 2) are, in the bore frame: wide (155, −20, 0) mm, detail (145, +40, 0) mm.
  A check against the 2026-10-02 registration: with those positions and parallel axes, the detail
  view's centre would sit at 0.493 of the wide frame across and 0.419 down for a subject at 1 m;
  measured 0.49 and 0.45. So the detail camera points about 4 mrad left of and 23 mrad below the
  wide one (about 1.3°, the step a swap shows today). The signs agree with the owner's offsets; the
  pointing difference is what the zero measures.
- **Pointing (R_c):** measured by the zero.
- **Roll** is not modelled. The crosshair point is exact regardless; roll only displaces points away
  from it. The cameras' roll relative to each other was measured within 0.5° (2026-10-02).
- **The detail principal point** is assumed at its picture centre. An error there behaves like a
  pointing offset, which the zero absorbs.

### D2. Storage: the record's bore part

`bore.yaml` already holds per camera the installation mm, the base angles and the trims (effective
pointing = base + trims). The record is where the payload's facts live (owner), so:

- **The installation offsets are set per record**, on the page, in the ZERO procedure's first step
  (`set_bore_offset <wide|detail> <right_mm> <up_mm> <forward_mm>`).
- **A zero writes the trims** so that the effective pointing is the solved one, and records, per
  camera, the zero's distance and date. The bore part becomes `zeroed <date> at <D> m`.
- Clicks after a tap are ordinary trims (D9). #1 stays locked.

### D3. The detail camera is zeroed like the wide one

No new lens calibration. The measured view scale (5.90) carries a detail tap into the wide frame,
where the model already works (fact 2). The detail rows of D9 are enabled: trims, clicks, and the
tap. At 0.12 mrad per pixel the detail camera is the finer of the two, so it is zeroed first.

### D4. The tracker reasons as the camera that saw the frame

For a `narrow_view` frame, controld re-expresses each detection as the wide camera would have seen
it: through the detail camera's pointing and position, then the wide camera's, at the working depth.
Then the estimator, the world bearing and the aim agree for both cameras, and a camera swap no
longer steps. Without D4, the zero fixes the crosshairs but the tracker still puts the target on the
payload's line of sight only when the wide camera is on the main display.

### D5. Distance

- **The zero's distance is typed in metres** (the known distance to the target), replacing the
  5–1500 m ladder for the zero.
- **The working distance is separate (owner).** It is the tracker's assumed range and the distance
  the crosshairs are drawn for (today's ZERO AT, `alignment.assumed_depth_m`). A zero sets it to the
  zero's distance; it can be changed afterwards without a new zero, because with real installation
  offsets the zero holds at every distance and only the parallax t_c/D changes. A rangefinder will
  supply it later.

### D6. The procedure (MENU › CALIBRATION › ZERO)

On a record other than #1, BORE ON, in a MANUAL hold (owner: only there):

0. **Offsets:** check, or enter, each camera's installation offset for this record's payload.
1. **Distance:** type the target's distance.
2. **Point the payload** at the target with the D-pad. The page shows when the turret has settled
   (fact 8). Check that the payload is still on the target before tapping.
3. **Detail camera** (on the main display): tap the target. The crosshair jumps there. Fine clicks
   (0.1 mrad is about 0.8 detail pixels).
4. **Wide camera** (swap; nothing moves): tap the same target, then clicks.
5. **Save:** both cameras' pointing, the distance and the date go into the record (D2). The panel
   says ZEROED.

Commands:
- `zero_bore <wide|detail> <metres> <u> <v>`: the tap, in the wide frame (the page converts a
  detail tap with the view scale), at the target's distance;
- `set_bore_offset <wide|detail> <right_mm> <up_mm> <forward_mm>`;
- `set_bore_range <metres|inf>`: the working distance (ADR-005's `set_bore_zero`, renamed with
  `bore.yaml`'s `zero_m` → `range_m`; the old key is still read);
- D9's `trim_bore`, for both cameras.

`zero_bore` is allowed only in a MANUAL hold (owner). The others keep D9's rule (a MANUAL hold or
Shutdown). All need the bore on and refuse #1.

### D7. How a zero is checked

- **By construction:** each crosshair lands where it was tapped (unit test of D1's round trip).
- **On the station, with the owner and the video:** AUTO_TRACK a subject at the zero's distance.
  The subject must sit on the crosshair in both cameras, with no step at a swap. The page records the
  tracked anchor's distance from the crosshair after it settles, per camera, in mrad.

## 5. Risks

1. **Installation offsets wrong.** The zero is still exact at its distance; elsewhere the crosshair
   is off by Δt/D (10 mm at 10 m is 1 mrad). The owner's are rounded ("about 20 mm"): a 5 mm
   error is 0.5 mrad at 10 m and 0.05 mrad at 100 m.
2. **The payload's pointing is the operator's.** The zero is as good as the payload's own aim on
   the target when the tap is made, which fact 8 can spoil by a few mrad.
3. **Roll** (D1). Bounded by the 0.5° measured between the cameras; it matters only away from the
   crosshair.
4. **D4 changes the tracker's input** whenever the detail camera is on the main display. While a
   record's two cameras have identical offsets (every record today, from the placeholders) it is the
   identity, so it can be deployed before the zero without changing anything. Entering real offsets
   without zeroing would move detail-camera tracking by the cameras' parallax alone (60 mm is
   6 mrad at 10 m): the ZERO procedure therefore asks for the offsets and the taps together.
