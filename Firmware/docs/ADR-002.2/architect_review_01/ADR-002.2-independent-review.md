# ADR-002.2 — Independent yaw modelling review

Date: 1 October 2026  
Scope: supplied `ADR-002.2-yaw-physics-math-20261001.zip`; offline analysis only.  
Status: **yaw remains unqualified; no replacement model or controller is qualified by this review. Candidate14 was not physically run.**

## Executive decision

Stop trying to repair this result by running the same gain calculation against another coefficient fit of the same restricted model. Preserve the captures. Repair the identification contract: distinguish actuator input, mechanical state, friction state and sensor output; establish their clocks; and validate complete forward trajectories on genuinely separate runs.

The archive already acknowledges the failures. It also contains valuable engineering work: successful-TX histories, raw CAN bytes and native IMU samples, independent encoder confirmation of motion, retained rejected fits, and explicit refusal to call Candidate14 physically qualified. The main issue is not that an optimizer failed to converge. The latest optimizer terminated after 43 evaluations. Its result nevertheless fails 28 of 29 velocity reconstruction windows and eight angle windows.

The evidence establishes that the current combination of model, timing assumptions, calibration and fit domain is inadequate. It does **not** uniquely establish whether the dominant physical cause is Stribeck friction, presliding/hysteresis, spatial load, current-to-torque dynamics, sensor delay, or mechanical compliance. These must be separated by model comparison and discriminating experiments, rather than assumed.

This is a correction to the reusable Stage 1 architecture, not permission to make every subsequent payload calibration a fresh engineering redesign. Stage 2 should automatically identify parameters and choose among predeclared admissible structures. There remains one complete delivery standard, including physical Stage 3a and production Stage 3b validation.

## 1. Scope and independently reproduced findings

The companion `audit.py` reads the supplied ZIP directly. It requires only Python, NumPy and pandas, and neither project source nor hardware. `reproduced/audit_summary.json`, `reproduced/audit_runs.csv` and `reproduced/fit_window_inventory.csv` are its executed outputs.

### E1 — Raw records are useful, not merely an untrustworthy decoder output

All 25 physical journals were read. Their event counts match `EXPERIMENT_INDEX.json`. Across 286,319 paired yaw raw-CAN/decoded feedback records, the encoder, integer RPM and signed current fields match a separate byte decoder, with zero mismatches or unpaired decoded records. Reconstructing encoder movement from wrapped 8192-count values agrees with logged relative movement to floating-point precision.

For Candidate13, applying the supplied frozen gyro projection to the native gyro samples reproduces the logged observation in all 1,449 control cycles; maximum absolute difference is approximately 1.11e-16 rad/s. This checks implementation arithmetic. It does not validate the physical mounting calibration, sensor filter, current meaning, or clock semantics.

### E2 — The fixed 60.593 ms delay needs reopening

A descriptive fit of decoded current against successful transmitted current was performed:

`I_feedback(t) = gain * u_successful_TX(t - lag) + offset`.

The reproducible audit searches integer lags from 0 through 150 ms. Every lag uses the same valid interval, excluding the first 150 ms after the first successful TX. The best lag is 1 ms in 24 runs and 2 ms in one run. In Candidate13, the fitted gain is approximately 0.99994. With unity gain and zero offset, the RMS telemetry discrepancy is about 0.003108 A at 1 ms versus 0.053169 A at the frozen 60.593 ms delay.

This is **not** proof of a 1 ms physical torque response. The waveform is correlated and slew-limited, the search resolution is 1 ms, current-feedback filtering/semantics remain unqualified, and kernel acceptance is not measured hardware application time. In particular, a reported current can be a proxy for the regulated quantity rather than a calibrated torque-producing current.

It is, however, strong evidence against treating 60.593 ms as an established current-delivery delay. Keep command-to-current dynamics, mechanical breakaway delay and measurement latency separate. Use the independently measured current as an additional observation. Do not silently replace 60.593 ms with 1 ms in the controller.

### E3 — The latest fit uses 4.309 seconds from three physical runs

The final update has 29 fitted MOVE segments. Their durations total 4.308642366 s; the median is 59.985071 ms, with 16 of 29 at or below 61 ms. The three source journals are Candidate12 out/back, Candidate12 +5 degrees/s, and Candidate13 +5 degrees/s. Many small windows contain only three or four native gyro samples.

The 29 updated prediction IDs are exactly the 29 training IDs. These are **in-sample reconstruction tests**, not 29 independently held-out physical experiments. Independent encoder/gyro observations are valuable, but independent sensors do not make a training trajectory an independent validation trajectory.

The 43 integral equations contain 38 positive-direction and five negative-direction equations. Only eight coordinates of the full 18-coordinate posture-tied representation are observed. Their rank is eight and normalized condition is 22.936. This says the chosen observed linear columns are numerically distinguishable in that reduction. It does not establish global identifiability, correct model structure, or predictive accuracy.

Yaw support spans 31.2890625 degrees. Pitch support spans approximately 0.025514 degrees. Tied pitch rows and unobserved map cells must remain explicitly prior-only; they do not constitute measured posture or global-domain coverage.

### E4 — Coefficients absorb unresolved effects

Recent fitted inertia-equivalent coefficients range from 0.0340195 to 0.2634202 A*s^2/rad, a factor of approximately 7.74. The latest value is 0.22412855. Several viscous coefficients sit effectively at zero under a nonnegative bound.

These are competing estimates, not measurements that the mechanical inertia changed by a factor of 7.74. The input-to-acceleration coefficient is a lumped quantity, affected by physical inertia, effective torque gain and current scale. It can also be distorted by wrong input delay, a missing friction term, noisy regressors and insufficiently separated trajectories.

The supplied positive-direction residual analysis reports a load-residual correlation of -0.84890 with speed, versus -0.44522 with angle. At a burst-window average of 20.631 degrees/s, the model's load-equivalent current is about 0.56239 A, compared with about 0.43173 A inferred under that model's inertial assumptions. This supports testing speed-dependent or history-dependent load. It does not uniquely prove a friction law; the residual inherits the assumptions used to calculate it.

The exploratory integral fits in this review also change materially when delay and velocity dependence change. They are sensitivity calculations, not a reproduction of the original output-error optimizer, and not qualified replacements. Their equation errors are not comparable to the supplied normalized output-error scores.

### E5 — Candidate13 has a real tracking failure, despite its acceptable mean

| Continuous-motion fraction | 0.896 | >=0.95 |

Later drift after actual current reaches zero is a different metric; its approximately 0.13184 degree pass does not erase the zero-reference drift failure.

Positive motor current during an overspeed event is not, by itself, proof of incorrect braking: the net accelerating torque depends on opposing load. Likewise, arithmetic replay of the controller does not identify the mechanical cause of the oscillation.

### E6 — Candidate14 is diagnostic output, not a hardware recommendation

The retained handoff reports 54/54 failed formal linear checks and 270/270 failed native motion simulations across its retained alternatives. Twelve margins are undefined after failed poles. The worst spectral radius is approximately 1.0493253. The nine models, including nominal, are conflicting descriptive alternatives, not a statistically calibrated confidence ensemble.

Candidate14 was never physically run. Retain its rejection evidence. No gains from this review are authorized for deployment.

## 2. What went wrong in the identification contract

The fitted moving model is

`a(z) * q_ddot + b(z) * q_dot + h(q,z,d) = u_successful_TX(t - delay)`.

Its directional, position-dependent `h` can represent a useful steady moving load map. It cannot by itself distinguish rest from sliding, model presliding memory, distinguish a just-reversed contact from established motion, or represent velocity weakening. A separate measured start-current interval does not solve this unless the complete simulator connects that interval to a stateful start/stop transition model.

The observations of interest repeatedly traverse precisely these regimes. Fitting only established MOVE windows can initialize a running model, but cannot qualify a model for startup, low-speed stick-slip, reversal, or stopping. Resetting the simulated state at each short window can also hide cumulative error and erase actuator/friction state history. The supplied model already fails many of those short in-sample windows.

The agent has already used sensible components—integral initialization, actual successful pre-window TX history, bounded output-error fitting, whitening and Huber loss. Recommending the same optimizer ingredients again would miss the issue. The next work must address the physical structure, timing, experiment design and estimation assumptions.

Closed-loop operation is another qualification: the controller input depends on measured motion and measurement noise. A regression that treats noisy states and feedback-generated inputs as exact can be biased. Use a suitable closed-loop prediction-error/noise model, instrumental-variable method, or other justified estimator; validate the chosen estimator on synthetic closed-loop data with the actual sampling, saturation and sensor errors. Output-error fitting is not automatically unbiased merely because it is nonlinear or robust.

## 3. Correct way to consume the existing data

### 3.1 Preserve provenance and establish a canonical event schema

Each normalized record should retain archive member, original line, physical run ID, configuration ID, sensor/calibration revision, units, clock identity, timestamp provenance, generation/reset counter and validity flags. Derived values must retain their parents. Distinguish actual captures from replay, simulation, initialization and inherited priors.

Keep requested current, post-limit/post-slew current, successful kernel-accepted current and decoded motor-current feedback as separate channels. Reconstruct successful TX as zero-order hold, with the full pre-window history. Do not use requested current as the plant input, interpolate current steps into ramps, or set pre-window input to zero when an actual earlier command is known.

### 3.2 Separate clocks and coordinate systems

Use device/sample time where its mapping to the common monotonic clock is established. Retain receive and dequeue times to audit transport delays, but do not silently substitute them for sample times. Estimate or bound offset, drift, timestamp uncertainty and reset handling. Passive pitch receive-time ZOH does not establish its device sample time.

Native gyro samples are approximately 50 Hz, controller cycles approximately 200 Hz, and yaw encoder receipts approximately 1 kHz. Reusing one gyro observation in four controller cycles does not create four independent measurements. Preserve freshness explicitly. Offline smoothing may be acausal for identification, but online inference and prospective prediction may not use future data.

Unwrap the raw encoder per session, then establish a documented common shaft-phase registration. Keep shaft phase, accumulated winding, session-relative angle and world-frame angle separate. Early acquisitions use different local origins; a supplied normalization report already compensates some of these. The review is not claiming all earlier normalization was wrong. Several older feedback sessions differ from the common datum by one to four counts; the latest three fitted sessions align with that datum. Thus this is a pooling hazard, not an established cause of the latest failure.

The encoder step is 0.0439453125 degrees. One count across 5 ms implies 8.789 degrees/s; across 20 ms, 2.197 degrees/s. Integer RPM telemetry has a 6 degrees/s step. Neither a per-cycle encoder difference nor raw RPM is adequate as an exact low-speed velocity truth. Fit quantized position and native gyro observations through a measurement model; use longer-window encoder rates as explicitly bandwidth-limited independent checks.

### 3.3 Retain all regimes and censored observations

Label rest, positive and negative established motion, breakaway, deceleration, reversal, saturation, and uncertain transition intervals. Do not delete hard-to-fit samples to make the final model pass. It is legitimate to exclude a regime from a particular initialization while retaining it for joint identification and validation.

A failed start up to a tested current is an inequality/censored observation, not a measured equality for the breakaway threshold. A successful start gives an interval influenced by slew and timing. Keep direction, previous motion, dwell time, position, posture, temperature and payload attached to these events.

### 3.4 Split by physical run before selection and calibration

Use whole-run and, where available, repeated-session/day blocks for train/selection/validation. Do not randomly partition adjacent samples or windows from one controller burst. Estimate transformations, noise models, timing and any learned window rules without using the final holdout.

The retained 0.60-A run remains useful as a mechanical regression holdout, but it does not by itself establish low-speed performance. It was available to prior analysis and has been inspected in this review for telemetry consistency; it is not a genuinely unseen future experiment. New prospective runs are necessary.

### 3.5 Fit trajectories, not only equations

Keep integral initialization to avoid noisy acceleration differentiation. Then jointly estimate identifiable parameters and bounded initial latent states against native position, gyro and current observations. Use a physically justified sensor-noise model, channel scaling and robust losses. Multiple shooting is acceptable for optimization, but continuity constraints must connect segments. Unconstrained state resets must not be used to claim whole-run prediction.

Use run-block uncertainty estimation and parameter profile checks when the number of independent runs supports them. A covariance from thousands of correlated encoder receipts must not be presented as thousands of independent experiments. Do not inflate process noise until deterministic residual structure appears to pass.

## 4. Replacement model architecture

This is a **predeclared model family and selection procedure**, not a demand to fit every listed effect immediately or add unlimited coefficients.

### 4.1 Actuator layer

Model successful command delivery and regulated current separately. A testable family includes an algebraic current map or a bounded first-order response:

`T_i * d(i_eff)/dt + i_eff = g_i * u_TX(t - delta_i) + i_bias`.

Include documented saturation, slew, supply-voltage and temperature dependence only where required. A distinct observation equation maps effective current to reported current, with its own scale/filter/latency uncertainties. Compare this with the fast telemetry correspondence already present in the archive. Do not identify a precise motor electrical time constant from a slow slew waveform.

### 4.2 Mechanical layer and payload

For the complete two-axis system, use a physically structured parent model:

`M(q; pi_c) q_ddot + C(q,q_dot; pi_c) q_dot + g(q; pi_c,g_base) + tau_f(q,q_dot,xi,T;c) + tau_cable(q,chi;c) = B_tau K_t(T) i_eff + tau_disturbance`.

`c` identifies the mechanical setup; `pi_c` contains the supported mass, centre-of-mass and inertia combinations; `xi` is friction state; `chi` can represent cable winding/history. The kinematic transformation, mechanical transmission, frame conventions and signs must be explicit. The yaw-only fixed-pitch reduction is the immediate identification problem; the current data do not identify the full two-axis model.

Mass alone is insufficient. A payload at an increased radius changes axis inertia even if its mass is unchanged. Its axis inertia contribution is

`J_axis = e^T R I_COM R^T e + m * (||r||^2 - (e^T r)^2)`.

Doubling a point mass's perpendicular radius quadruples that point mass's contribution, not necessarily the total assembly inertia. For an ideal vertical yaw axis at fixed pitch, gravity does not create a direct yaw-axis torque simply because the payload is off-centre; it can still affect bearing loads, friction and the inertia distribution. Pitch, base tilt and axis geometry change the gravity/coupling problem.

Identify only supported parameter combinations. For a single axis, `a = J / K_effective` is identifiable under appropriate excitation, but `J` and `K_effective` are not separately determined by the same input/acceleration equation without an independent physical datum. A calibrated torque/current relation or a known added inertia can break that ambiguity under controlled conditions. Until then, retain honestly labelled A-equivalent parameters; they can still support useful control.

### 4.3 Friction and load layer

For local fixed-pitch yaw, a suitable reduction is

`a_c * v_dot = u_effective - load(q,c) - B_c*v - F_nonlinear(v,xi,q,T,c)`.

Separate signed spatial load from direction-dependent friction. One admissible moving-friction candidate is

`F_moving(v) = sign(v) * [F_c^d + (F_s^d-F_c^d)*exp(-(|v|/v_s^d)^p)] + B*v`,

with nonnegative physical parameters and `F_s^d >= F_c^d` where this family is selected. This is a candidate Stribeck form, not an already identified description of this hardware.

At rest, do not evaluate that expression with `sign(0)=0` and thereby erase holding friction. Use a true stick mode: friction balances the other torques within the directional static-friction interval, and a breakaway transition occurs when the admissible balance is exceeded. Model stopping and reattachment consistently.

If repeated approach/dwell/reversal tests demonstrate history effects, select a predeclared dynamic-friction candidate such as LuGre or a hybrid presliding model. Do not give each short training window an arbitrary friction offset or hidden-state reset: that destroys predictive meaning. Extra parameters must improve blocked, free-running validation and be observable in the available data.

A falling friction curve can have negative incremental slope while physical viscous friction remains nonnegative. Therefore the near-zero fitted `b` may be a restricted model trying to explain velocity weakening. Simply allowing an arbitrary negative constant viscous coefficient is not a general physical solution.

Spatial motor-related torque may be periodic, but cable winding, cable routing and some assembly loads need not repeat every revolution. Do not force all residual load into an eight-node periodic map. Keep conservative/load terms and dissipative terms separate enough to maintain meaningful interpolation and extrapolation rules.

### 4.4 Measurement layer

Represent encoder quantization and registration, gyro bias and coordinate projection, sensor filtering, timestamp mapping, and current-feedback semantics. The frozen single-yaw-column projection only covers its identified posture. Full two-axis operation requires the appropriate mounting/kinematic mapping.

Gyro and game rotation vector are not independent physical instruments simply because they are separate channels from the same IMU. Encoder evidence supplies a separate modality. If rotor angle and payload attitude later diverge systematically in bandwidth or direction, test a compliance/backlash/two-inertia alternative; do not add it without evidence.

### 4.5 Automatic family selection and acceptance

Stage 1 must predefine admissible model structures, constraints, selection criteria, uncertainty handling, data schemas and synthetic tests. Stage 2 supplies physical observations and chooses/estimates among these structures deterministically. Prefer the least complex structure that passes held-out trajectory tests; reject unsupported global claims.

There is no requirement that the final model identify every microscopic friction parameter. It must predict the control-relevant behaviour within a declared domain with an uncertainty small enough for the acceptance limits.

## 5. Additional experiments should discriminate, not just accumulate

First reuse the existing data to audit clocks/current semantics and compare the predefined structures. Then acquire only missing discriminating evidence, within validated speed, travel, thermal and current limits. A current cap alone does not bound motion safely when the model is uncertain.

| Missing distinction | Informative experiment | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| Electrical/current response versus motion onset | Bounded current modulation with precise TX/current clocks; separately measure motion | Separates regulated-current timing from stiction and sensor latency |
| Spatial load versus speed-dependent load | Revisit the same angle range at several speeds and both directions | Prevents one correlated trajectory from making angle imitate velocity |
| Inertia versus drag | Paired acceleration, coast and deceleration segments while already moving | Changes acceleration independently of steady load |
| Static versus sliding friction | Repeated ramps, dwell/restart, stop and reversal trials at matched positions | Identifies threshold intervals and history dependence |
| Posture/payload dependence | Designed posture grid and known payload configurations, with repeats | Establishes inertia, gravity/coupling and load changes rather than tying rows |
| Compliance or mounting motion | Compare rotor encoder and independently mapped payload attitude during suitable bounded excitation | Tests whether a rigid one-inertia model is adequate |

Do not increase unknown current limits merely because a trial did not start. Preserve censored outcomes. Check the feasibility of startup and slew constraints: the time needed to reach an unknown breakaway current, plus actuator/measurement/confirmation latency, places a conditional lower bound on startup. Do not call the highest tested failed-start current an exact threshold.

Fit/synthesis should return an explicit reasoned outcome: accepted within domain, more information needed, structurally rejected, or performance infeasible under the existing constraints. It must not return the last bandwidth-grid point as a deployable result when every point fails. Retaining such a point as labelled diagnostic evidence is fine.

## 6. Verification against future real-world prediction

### 6.1 Three distinct tests

**A. Input-driven plant validation.** Initialize from observations available at the start of a held-out run. Drive the plant with the actual successful input history and predict position, velocity and sensor outputs forward without injecting future measured motion. Evaluate the spatial/friction terms at predicted state, not at measured future angle. Do not reset every short segment. This validates the plant conditioned on realized inputs; it is not a pre-run closed-loop forecast.

**B. Closed-loop prospective simulation.** Before a physical trial, freeze the configuration, calibration, model, uncertainty set, controller revision and reference. Simulate the same controller with simulated sensor/actuator paths so it generates its own future current. Include current limits, slew, sample freshness, timing, observer, integration/anti-windup and rest/reversal transitions. Save timestamped predictions and bounds before receiving future measurements. Using the recorded future current here would make it a retrospective plant replay, not a prospective closed-loop forecast.

**C. Physical qualification.** Execute the frozen trial and compare with the saved predictions. Stage 3a must pass in the dedicated validation program. Stage 3b must also pass in production software using the same identified plant/measurement semantics and control core. Full delivery includes both axes and the promised configuration/domain coverage, not just the present yaw experiment.

The archive contains numerical controller replay, not source code. This review checks the records and supplied results but cannot independently establish the native implementation's complete correctness from this data-only package.

### 6.2 Metrics and horizons

Evaluate both short horizons relevant to feedback and complete manoeuvres. Candidate reporting horizons include 50, 100, 200 and 500 ms, 1 s, and full-run trajectories. These are analysis horizons, not relaxed acceptance limits.

Report angle and velocity errors, endpoint drift, startup timing, false starts, stall duration/continuity, reversal, settling, stop drift, overspeed, saturation and current predictions. Keep mean tracking separate from jitter; a burst/stall sequence can have the correct mean.

Retain the frozen quality targets listed in E5, the corresponding velocity-dependent limits, and the independent prediction angle RMS target of 0.15 degrees. Do not substitute eventual zero-current settling for the fixed two-second zero-reference requirement. The stated 30 degrees/s^2 reference shaping is guidance, not an independently demonstrated physical acceleration limit.

Inspect residuals against angle, speed, acceleration, current, direction, time since reversal/start, posture and temperature. Test innovation whiteness and appropriate orthogonality to past information/exogenous excitation. In a feedback loop, correlation with future control is not by itself a model failure because future control responds to prior measurement errors.

Assess uncertainty-band coverage **and** width. A sufficiently wide band can enclose any failed trajectory; it does not meet a 0.15-degree precision requirement. Do not label all rejected alternative fits a confidence set. Separate plausible identified uncertainty, deliberately stressed models, and hypotheses already contradicted by data.

### 6.3 Deployment record and applicability

A deployable configuration package should contain model-family version, hardware/firmware/motor mode, payload identity and geometry/inertia information, mounting/cable setup, sensor calibration, parameter estimates, supported domain, uncertainty, reference/limit definitions, evidence splits and physical 3a/3b results.

Reuse it without retuning while the setup remains inside that validated envelope. Temperature and modest load/friction changes can be covered by measured parameter scheduling or bounded robust uncertainty. A new payload distribution, altered cable path or major friction change outside the envelope triggers the reusable automatic identification and verification procedure—not manual PID experimentation and not a fresh architecture project.

## 7. Controller implications after model validation

Retain the distinction between model-based feedforward and feedback correction. Feedforward should use the appropriate inertial, moving-load and friction-state estimates; a breakaway term is not a constant that should be applied throughout established motion. Integrator and transition logic must be consistent with actual successful current, saturation and slew.

Controller synthesis must include the local incremental dynamics of the selected nonlinear model. If friction is velocity weakening, the incremental damping is not merely the nonnegative viscous coefficient `b`; it includes the derivative of the moving-friction law. The current gain equations cannot be justified for all regimes simply by inserting another fitted `a` and `b`.

Use nonlinear manoeuvre checks in addition to local linear margin checks. Revalidate the actual observer/sample/actuator implementation. If no controller meets the unchanged limits over the justified uncertainty set, report infeasibility and identify whether the limiting factor is sensing, current/slew, mechanical friction, model uncertainty or the required operating envelope.

## 8. Instructions for the local architect and tuning agent

1. Freeze and retain all current evidence. Keep Candidate14 nondeployable and yaw unqualified. Do not change acceptance criteria or replace the task with manual gain search.
2. Reproduce the raw-byte, gyro-projection, clock/current and training-window audit. Normalize coordinates with provenance; keep all regimes and censored starts.
3. Amend Stage 1 with the predeclared actuator/mechanics/friction/measurement model family, identification/selection methods, synthetic closed-loop tests and prospective verification contract. No physical measurements are required to define this architecture; Stage 2 supplies their values.
4. Refit and compare structures using existing data first. Report identifiable versus prior-only parameters, timing assumptions, blocked splits, residual structure and trajectory errors. Request targeted physical experiments only where the alternatives cannot be separated from existing observations.
5. Freeze a supported configuration-specific model before controller synthesis. If none passes predictive gates, return a model/data failure; do not hide it with broad uncertainty or more gain candidates.
6. Save predictions before new physical runs; compare full trajectories and unchanged quality targets. Complete both dedicated-program Stage 3a and production Stage 3b, with full promised axis/configuration coverage.

## 9. Limits of this review and scientific references

This review performs independent data/decoder/timing/fit-domain audits and exploratory numerical diagnostics. It does not perform physical experiments, identify a uniquely proven friction mechanism, calibrate physical torque/Iq, or supply qualified replacement gains. Original project source and independent original-file digests were not provided or checked against a repository. Reading the supplied archive does not establish equivalence to unseen originals.

Relevant primary references used for methodological checks:

- Russ Tedrake, *Underactuated Robotics*, Chapter 18, “System Identification”: equation versus simulation error, physical/lumped parameters and experiment design.
- Miguel Galrinho, Cristian Rojas and Håkan Hjalmarsson, “Estimating Models with High-Order Noise Dynamics Using Semi-Parametric Weighted Null-Space Fitting,” arXiv:1708.03947: closed-loop identification and noise-model issues.
- Carlos Canudas de Wit, Henrik Olsson, Karl Johan Åström and Pablo Lischinsky, “A New Model for Control of Systems with Friction,” IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control 40(3), 1995, DOI 10.1109/9.376053: dynamic friction model family commonly called LuGre.

The proposed architecture, experiments and acceptance contract above are recommendations derived for this supplied evidence, not claims that those references validate this particular hardware.
